# FORK-S3O — what overconstraint means, and how a mate-placed face is proven structural

## For Ev

**Recommendation (likely).** Treat a copy's bundle of mates as a system of equations in one unknown, the copy's frame, and let both halves of the fork fall out of that reading:

1. **Overconstraint.** A mate that pins nothing of its copy refuses, unmeasured. "Pins nothing" is decided by subgroup algebra alone: the mate's subgroup contains the fold of the bundle's *other* mates (so it can only agree or disagree with them, never narrow them). The solve never asks which of the two it is. A mate that does pin something is admitted; where admitting it needs a value decided Zero (the two-peg case below), that Zero is recorded as a coincidence for the `unproven-coincidence` lint, like a boolean's glue.
2. **A mate-placed face.** The door proves it with no rung of its own and no stored frame. A placed copy's frame is not a variable and never a value the door reads: the copy's poses enter the door as `Carried { copy, pose }`, and each mate of the bundle is a rewrite rule `Carried { copy, a } ≡ b (mod the kind's symmetry, with the sense)`. A relation between two copies is rewritten into one space, where it is a within-part relation that stage 4's `CarrierForm`s already decide.

Both say the same thing: *a placement is its mates, and nothing else*. A redundant-but-consistent mate would be a second rewrite rule for the same carried pose, whose consistency is exactly the value coincidence the door would then have to lint — which is the "constraint that falls back to an assertion" you rejected.

### Premise check

- The brief's reading of today's code is right: `coset::intersect` re-measures a non-lowering mate through `member_of` (the `TranslationInPlane` check for a parallel plane mate) and admits it when the offset agrees. It also re-measures the *held* side, which is met by construction; that re-measure is what the box-widening rows blame. Both go.
- D10's sentence "a mate added to a pinned copy refuses … without measuring" states only the pinned case. The question is the unpinned redundant case, and there the subgroup test *does* measure one thing: whether the two planes are parallel (the table's `parallel` predicate). That is a decided predicate like every case split of the fold, not a check of the mate's offset, and the text below says so rather than claiming "without measuring" for a case where it is false.
- The brief's example "a second plane mate on a parallel face, pinning nothing new" is not the only shape of redundancy. DOF-counting also calls the two-peg assembly overconstrained (two coaxial mates on parallel pins: the second removes one freedom but carries three conditions, parallelism and spacing). It must assemble. So "no measured agreement in the fold" is the wrong rule; "the mate lowers the fold" is the right one, with the agreements it carries recorded.

### Terms

- **Fold**: the coset intersection of a bundle's mates (A11 (1)); its **subgroup** is what the copy is still free to do.
- **Lowers**: a mate lowers a fold when the intersection's subgroup has smaller dimension than the fold's. In the closure the table is closed over, equal dimension means equal subgroup, so a mate that does not lower a fold has a subgroup *containing* it.
- **Redundant mate**: one whose subgroup contains the fold of the bundle's other mates. This is order-independent (leave-one-out), unlike "the mate added last lowers nothing", which would admit the bundle (Plane, Frame) and refuse (Frame, Plane) on the same face.
- **Carried pose**: `Carried { copy, pose }`, a pose of the copy's part, in the part's seed space, as the copy carries it. The copy's own side of a mate is a pose of the *part*; the door reads it as carried by the copy being placed. The partner side is `OfCopy { copy′, pose′ }`, which is `Carried { copy′, pose′ }` by another name, or a pose of the seed or the world.

### 1. Overconstraint, as a final state

**The door** (insert and solve alike, A11 (1)'s split kept) refuses:

- `Overconstrained { placement, mate, held }` for any bundle holding a redundant mate. On a pinned copy no predicate runs (`Trivial ∩ G = Trivial` is a table row with no measurement). On an unpinned copy the dimension test asks the table's case splits (parallel, perpendicular, point-on-line) and nothing about offsets. The refusal names every redundant mate; the recourse is to delete it or, if the user meant a check, stage 5's `Assert` on the gap.
- `Contradictory { held, added, predicate, clash }` for a lowering mate whose coset does not meet the fold (two axis mates on skew lines at the wrong distance), with the added mate's measured clash, as today.
- `Under { residual }` for a bundle whose fold is not `Trivial` (unchanged; rung (c) unbuilt). An empty bundle is its own space, not a verdict.

**The solve** does the same and nothing more:

- A consistent redundant mate and an inconsistent one are *the same refusal*. The kernel does not tell them apart, because telling them apart is measuring the mate's offset against a pose the other mates already fixed — a mate checking, which D10 forbids.
- A lowering mate is admitted by constructing the representative to meet the held side exactly (never re-measured) and deciding only the conditions the construction did not solve for. Each table row states which of the added subgroup's constraints it meets by construction and which it decides, as `CarrierFlow` states a verb's carriers. A decided condition at Zero admits the mate and is **recorded** as a `Coincidence { site: MateFold, relation: the predicate's name, margin }` on the placement, in decision order; definite refuses `Contradictory`; the sliver band escalates `Indeterminate`. Nothing about the fold's algorithm changes.

**A11 (1)'s outcome list** becomes: per bundle, PINNED (`Trivial`), UNDER (the residual named), CONTRADICTORY (two lowering mates whose cosets do not meet, the added mate's clash quoted), OVERCONSTRAINED (a mate whose subgroup contains the fold of the rest, named, nothing measured). "Exact coset intersection" reads "decided coset intersection".

**Worked example.** A plate with two parallel pins into a base with two holes, pin spacing `s`, hole spacing `t`. Mate 1 (Axis–Axis): `Se3 → Cylindrical`. Mate 2: `Cylindrical ∩ Cylindrical`, parallel and distinct, `→ Prismatic` (lowers 2 → 1), and the row decides spacing: `|s − t|` Zero admits and records one `MateFold` row; the lint proves it iff `s` and `t` are one linear form (one variable read twice), else reports "pin spacing `s` meets hole spacing `t` only at the current values", recourse "make the slot reading `t` read `s`". A third Plane mate on the plate's foot, normal along the pins: `Prismatic ∩ Planar`, perpendicular `→ Trivial`: pinned. A fourth Plane mate on the plate's top, parallel to the foot: `Trivial` against anything, no predicate, `Overconstrained { held: Trivial }` naming it. Reorder the fourth before the third and the leave-one-out test still names the top mate (given the other three it narrows nothing) — not the foot mate.

**What this makes true that the alternatives cannot.** A bundle never holds a mate that says nothing; a mate's meaning does not depend on where it sits; the fold measures only what it must and records every such Zero, so an assembly that holds only at today's values is reported where it is decided, and the box lanes stop widening on residuals met by construction. What it refuses that one might want: writing "and this face is flush too" as a mate. That statement is an assertion and has stage 5's door.

**Alternatives.** (a) *Admit a redundant mate when its offset agrees* (today, respelled). Pros: the user can over-say a placement. Cons: a mate is a placing statement or a check depending on the rest of the bundle; the check is re-measured at every solve on every lane; and the agreement it checks is a value coincidence the door must lint anyway, so the mate adds a second spelling of `Assert`. This is the state Ev's "worse than either" names. (b) *Admit a redundant mate only when it is structurally implied* (the door's canonical forms prove the second plane is the first's construction). Cons: the solve would read stage 4's door, inverting the layering, to admit a mate that places nothing and, being structural, has no finding to quiet: a mechanism nothing needs. (c) *The order-dependent rule* (the mate added last must lower the held fold). Cons: bundle order decides the verdict at load; (Plane, Frame) passes and (Frame, Plane) fails. Reversible: all three are relaxations of the recommendation and can be added later; the recommendation cannot be reached from (a) without dropping admitted mates (the migration F already plans this).

### 2. A mate-placed face, as a final state

**The door's reading of a bundle.** A pinned copy `C` with mates `(a_i, b_i, S_i, sense_i)` contributes the rules `Carried { C, a_i } ≡ b_i (mod S_i)`, orientation flipped by the sense. The targets `b_i` lie in earlier copies, the seed or the world (the door's acyclicity), so rewriting toward targets terminates and the normal form is unique — *because* no bundle holds two rules for one carried pose (part 1).

**Proving a relation between copies.** A row's relation (`SameOpposite`, `Tangent`, `OnCarrier`, …) is, in stage 4 C's forms, an equality of pose forms modulo a symmetry plus linear-form offsets. The door rewrites one side's carried poses through the mates until both sides are forms in one space, then decides it as a within-part relation. Three cases:

- **The mated face itself.** `C`'s cap is `Project { FaceFrame(cap), Plane }`; a Plane mate reads exactly that pose, so `Carried { C, cap } mod Planar` rewrites to the partner's plane form in one step. Proven, `Rung::CanonicalForm`.
- **A face derived from the mated one.** A fillet flank on the cap: the fillet's `CarrierFlow` states the flank's axis as the cap plane shifted by `−r` along its normal, direction along the edge. The at-rest census finds the flank tangent to the base plate. `Tangent(cylinder, plane)` is `distance(axis, plane) = r` and `direction ⟂ normal`, both forms over the cap plane's form plus `r`; rewriting `Carried { C, cap }` to the plate's plane makes the first `r = r` and the second the fillet's own construction. Proven, with nothing said about fillets at the door. The same holds for any carrier the part's forms express relative to a mated pose modulo what that mate pins: a side wall of `C` against a plane mate alone is *not* proven (the mate leaves in-plane slide), which is right.
- **A carrier that needs the whole frame.** A vertex of `C` on a vertex of `D`, both pinned by three plane mates to `B`. The vertex is the meet of three of `C`'s faces (a within-part fact, rung 2 or rung 3); each face rewrites to a plane of `B`; equality is equality of the three `B` forms. Where the part's forms cannot express the carrier through the mated poses, the row stays unproven at rung 2 and goes to rung 3.

**Rung 3** is the same replay as for a boolean: the solve at `Sym` with every variable a symbol gives each copy's frame as a polynomial in the variables, and the census margin's normal form decides. It needs `Sym` to be a `SolveScalar`.

**The world.** A mate to the world rewrites to `Offset { World, … }` forms. Two plates placed to the world at one height variable `h` are proven coincident; at two typed `10 mm` they are not. Stage 4's test 22 stands.

**What stage 3 must represent for this to hold.** (i) A mate's copy side is a pose of the *part*, in its seed space; "as the copy carries it" is the door's `Carried`, not a stored value. (ii) The partner side is `OfCopy { copy, pose }` with `pose` in that copy's part space, or a seed or world pose. (iii) `Place` defines its `Body` and no `Frame` port: a solved frame lives in `SolvedPoses` as a value that evaluation and export read and the door never does. A `Frame` output of `Place` would be an opaque atom that proves only "same copy" — stage 4 C's interim, made permanent. (iv) Every number a mate carries is in a pose definition (an `Offset`), so it is a linear form the rewrite composes; `PlanarRest.offset` and the clocking rider as raw floats would be invisible to the door. (v) The bundle's targets form a DAG (already the door's acyclicity rule). Nothing else: no "placed" pose arm, no frame variable per copy, no theorem per mate kind.

**What it costs.** Stage 3: the leave-one-out fold at the door and at the solve (`n` extra folds per bundle, `n ≤ 3` in practice), the per-row statement of met-by-construction vs decided constraints, a `MateFold` record and its emission. Stage 4: H becomes "rewrite through bundles" rather than "compose a frame"; B's `DecisionSite` gains `MateFold`; the lint's recourse gains "place one relative to the other" for two carried atoms whose chains never meet. Nothing is stored twice; the solve stays over decided predicates; the door stays one place.
