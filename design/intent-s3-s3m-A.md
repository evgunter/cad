# FORK-S3M — a placement, what a mate reads, and a multi-body instance

## For Ev

**Recommendation (one design, three parts).**

1. **A placement is one node, `Place { shape, mates }`, and a mate is a clause of it, not a node.** `shape` reads one `Body` or one `Bodies`; `mates` is an ordered list; the node defines `copy` (the shape's own kind) and `frame: Frame`, the copy's frame. Each mate is `{ on, to }`: `on` a pose defined over the shape (its faces, or the frame its space was built in), `to` a pose of the space the copy goes into. The bundle folds to the copy's residual `Subgroup`; `Trivial` pins it. Nothing else places. A world placement is a `Place` whose `to` poses are in the world's space (directly or through other copies), and the product is every copy in that space.
2. **A mate equates two poses of one kind, modulo that kind's `Subgroup`. It carries nothing else.** `MatePrimitive`, `MateFrame`, `FrameBase`, `AxisSense`, `PlanarRest.offset` and the clocking rider all retire into pose definitions; the sense becomes a construction (`Flip`), not an operand.
3. **An instance defines `bodies: Bodies` (the part's product, in one space) and `frame: Frame` (the part's world frame, carried as a pose). A placement reads `bodies` whole.** FORK-1's "one `Body` per world placement" changes to this. One body of it is DM3's index.

Confidence: `sure` on 1's directedness and on mates living inside the node; `likely` on `frame` as an output, on `Flip`, and on 3.

**Premise check.** The brief is right that D10 retires the spanning tree, and right that FORK-1's instance signature is in play. Two corrections. First, "a placement reads one `Body`" (D10 Operations, A10) and "a mate defines none" were designer text approved in passing in #4220 and #4222, not Ev's words; Ev's own sentence is the Spaces one ("a bundle of relations that together pin one copy of a part relative to others"), which this design keeps. Second, question 3's "the part's world frame relates them, and only export reads world coordinates" is not a tension: the instance exposes the part's world **frame** as a pose (a relation to the part's bodies), never its coordinates, exactly as a seed is a frame without coordinates.

### 1. What a placement is

**Terms.** A *space* is a set of copies related to one another (D10). A *seed* is a document's construction frame, valueless (stage 3 A). Every pose and shape variable has a *space*, derived from its reads: the seed's, the world's, an instance's, or a placement's. A *copy* is what has a pose; a body (shape) has none.

- **Why the mate is inside the node.** A mate's `on` side is a pose of the shape *before* it is anywhere: "the top face of the bracket". That pose has no meaning in the target space until the copy's frame carries it, and the frame is what the bundle is being solved for. So a mate as a free-standing node would read a pose that is only meaningful as "carried by this copy", and the copy would have to find its mates by a reverse lookup: a dependency that is not a read, which D10 abolished (it is A12's reading edge again). Inside the node, `on` reads a pose over the shape (an input), `to` reads a pose in the target space, and the DAG stays acyclic. A mate has no identity outside its placement, and that is the semantics: "the placement isn't a hole on the part to be filled, it's a description of how two inherently-unplaced parts relate" (Ev, 2026-10-03). A mate is addressed as (placement, position); the fold runs in bundle order, so the solve is deterministic (D9).
- **Directed, so no tree.** Which side moves is the `Place`'s `shape`. The user decides by which copy they place against which. A cycle cannot be written (reads are acyclic), so "an inconsistent loop" is unrepresentable; its closing mate lands in a pinned bundle and is an overconstraint (A11 (1), stage 3 F).
- **`frame: Frame` is an output.** It is where the shape's space frame lands: the seed for a local body, the instance's `frame` for its bodies, a copy's `frame` for a copy of a copy. It is a pose in the target space, not a location, and it is what a `Bodies` placement has that no single face names: "where the instance sits". A later placement reads it on its `to` side (`on: seed, to: InFrame { P.frame, 10 mm }` is "another one, 10 mm over"), and stage 4 H compares copies through it.
- **Deleting.** `RemoveMate` shrinks a bundle: an empty bundle is a copy in its own space (A11 (2)'s rule, Ev #3441), a non-trivial fold faults the placement `Under`. Deleting a `Place` deletes its mates with it and leaves readers of `copy` and `frame` unresolved, typed (D10's deletion rule). Deleting the shape strands the placement (A10 kept). No cascade, no refusal.
- **The world placement** (`PlaceInWorld { body, pose }`) is spelled `Place { shape: body, mates: [Frame: on = seed, to = InFrame { World, pose }] }`; the identity placement has an empty chain. The world is a valueless `Frame` that may stand only on a `to` side, never on `on` and never under a construction. A `Transform` is the same node with `to = InFrame { seed, chain }`: a copy moved within its own space.
- **Space is decided at the door.** Each `Place` requires its `on` poses in `shape`'s space and its `to` poses in one space; a boolean's operands in one space (D10); a pose combining two spaces refuses. All of it reads only definitions, no geometry.

### 2. What a mate reads

`Mate { on: S, to: S }`, two pose variables of one kind: `Frame`–`Frame` coincides, `Axis`–`Axis` is coaxial, `Plane`–`Plane` is coplanar; `Point` and `Direction` when `Subgroup` grows their arms (Ev, #4222: one `Subgroup` is a pose's symmetry and what a mate folds).

- **The numbers live in pose definitions.** A stand-off `h` is a `Plane` written in the partner's face frame at `z = h` (`InFrame { frame: FaceFrame { face }, coords }`, `h` an anonymous `Length`); a roll `θ` is a `Frame` turned about `z` by an anonymous `Angle`; a `MateFrame` on a part base is `InFrame { seed, chain }`, on a face base `InFrame { FaceFrame { face }, chain }`; the primitive picks the projection. So no mate holds a float, and every mate scalar is a slot (VR4, VR8).
- **The sense is a construction, not an operand.** `AxisSense` retires into `Flip { pose }`: the same plane with the opposite normal, the reversed axis or direction. A rest is `on: Plane(face a), to: Flip(Plane(face b))`. Reasons: the relation is bare equality, like every other relation D10 keeps ("an incidence is a construction over one variable, never a check between two"); a `Frame`–`Frame` "opposed" today picks a half-turn about `x` inside the kernel (`opposed()`), where the user now writes the turn they mean (`turn/2`, an exact constant, VR5); and `Flip` does not exist for a `Point`, so the sense-on-a-point table gap is a type error. A discrete `Sense` variable would be a slot nobody shares and a gap for `Point`.
- **A clocked coaxial is two mates.** `Coaxial` plus the rider pins spin and leaves slide: that residual (Prismatic) is not a kind, so it is `Axis`–`Axis` plus `Direction`–`Direction` over the two reference directions, one turned by `θ`. That needs `Direction`'s subgroup (translations and spin about it, dim 4) in the fold table in stage 3 B, which the shared `Subgroup` makes one arm.
- **Retired:** `Alignment`, `MateFrame`, `FrameBase`, `MatePrimitive`, `AxisSense`, `PlanarRest.offset`, the rider, `table_gap`, `MateFrame::authored` (its vectors become `InFrame` coordinates). `class` stays until stage 4 I.
- **Pose definitions needed:** `Seed`, `World`, `InFrame { frame, coords }` (the rigid chain is its `Frame` form, so `Offset` is not a second kind), `Carry { by: <a copy's frame>, pose }` (a pose of the shape as the copy carries it; a face of a copy needs none, since `Select(copy, name)` already reads it at its carried place), `Project`, `Flip`, `FaceFrame`, and the named combinations.

### 3. A multi-body instance

- **The unit of placement is the part's product.** A part document's product is its copies in its world's space, related by the placements the part wrote. Placing them one `Body` at a time either loses that relation or restates it in the assembly, which is saying twice what the part said once. A `Bodies` is exactly "several bodies in one space" (D10), and a pattern's output already is one; so `InstantiatePart { doc_ref, interface }` defines `bodies: Bodies` and `frame: Frame`. `Place` over a `Bodies` defines a `Bodies` copy, as `Transform` does today (stage 2 A).
- **`frame`** is the part's world frame as a pose in the instance's space: the one pose the part declared, the natural `on` side of a placement, and the analogue of a local body's seed. Its coordinates are never read: it is a relation to the part's bodies.
- **Picking one body** is DM3's index (`Count`, fixed by the pin), and a face of it is a `Select` over that body. A caller that wants the bracket's second body in a boolean places the instance first (relating the spaces, D10 Booleans), then indexes the copy.
- **FORK-1's signature changes**: "an instance of a part defines one `Body` variable per world placement of the part" becomes "defines its product, one `Bodies` whose count is the part's world placement count, and the part's world frame, a `Frame`, both in the instance's own space". Per-body ports would be sugar for the index; left out.
- **What this makes true that the others cannot:** one placement of an instance is one node; the part's internal relations survive the seam untouched; patterning an instance patterns its `bodies`.

### What each choice makes true, and reversibility

- Mates inside the node: no reverse lookup, no tree, no roots, no `Declaring`; a pinned copy cannot acquire a mate silently. Reversible only by reintroducing the reading-edge class; the reverse is the bad state.
- Equality-only mates with constructed sense: a mate is one thing; every number is a slot; the table has no gaps by type. Reversible (a sense slot could be added) but nothing would want it.
- `frame` as an output: stage 4 H's comparison and the `Bodies` placement have a variable to read. Reversible: drop the port and spell `Carry` by copy.
- `bodies: Bodies`: the part's relations are the part's. Reversible by adding per-body ports as sugar.

**Migration** (one-time, bit-checked at stage 3 C): a gauge becomes a named `Frame`, `InFrame { parent or World, placement }`; a group root becomes `Place { shape: I.bodies, mates: [I.frame ↔ InFrame { gauge frame, offset }] }`; each tree mate goes into the bundle of the copy today's BFS made its child, `on` over the child's body, `to` over the parent copy's face; declaring mates go into the later copy's bundle as the interim verified mates stage 3 C describes, and are dropped named at F; non-root offsets are dropped and named (stage 5's `Assert`); `PlaceInWorld` and `Transform` as above; `Opposed` becomes `Flip` on the `to` side, or an explicit half-turn for a `Frame` pair.

**Ratified text that changes**, quoted:

- D10 Operations: "an instance of a part defines one `Body` variable per world placement of the part" → "an instance of a part defines its product, a `Bodies`, and the part's world frame, a `Frame`"; "an assertion or a mate defines none" → "an assertion defines none; a mate is a clause of a placement, not an operation"; "A world placement is an operation reading one `Body` and defining its copy" → "A placement is an operation reading one shape (`Body` or `Bodies`) and a bundle of mates, defining the copy, of the shape's kind, and the copy's frame; a world placement is one whose mates reach the world's space".
- D10 Spaces: "the bundle of mates that pins one copy of a part relative to others" → "... one copy of a shape relative to poses of other copies, of the construction frame, or of the world: each mate equates a pose of the shape with a pose of the space the copy is placed into". "A mate places and never checks" stands.
- D10 Variables: "(a side, a half, a sense)" — no mate reads a sense; keep the kind only if another slot wants it.
- A3: the node vocabulary becomes `InstantiatePart { doc_ref, interface }`, `Place { shape, mates }`, and a mate equating two poses; "Mates are declarations" and "Every number that says where the two sides meet lives in the sides' offsets" go.
- A10: "reading one `Body`" → "reading one shape"; "the copies the document's world placements define" → "every copy in the world's space".
- A11 (1): "Each primitive pins the pair's relative pose" → "a mate's kinds name its coset"; `AxisSense`'s sentence goes. A11 (2), (3), (4) retire as stage 3's spec quotes them.
- FORK-1 (#4222) signature, as above. DM3 gains the instance's product as a `Bodies` picked by index.

**Worked example.** Bracket `K` (a part with base and clip bodies) on plate `P`, rested and pinned: `Place { shape: K.bodies, mates: [ Plane: on = Plane(FaceFrame(Select(index(K.bodies, 0), "bottom"))), to = Flip(Plane(FaceFrame(Select(Pplace.copy, "top")))); Axis: on = Axis(FaceFrame(Select(index(K.bodies, 0), "hole"))), to = Axis(FaceFrame(Select(Pplace.copy, "pin"))) ] }`. The fold is Planar ∩ Cylindrical with the axis along the normal → Revolute; a third mate on the clip's slot face pins it. A fourth refuses as an overconstraint. The clip rides along because it is in `K.bodies`.
