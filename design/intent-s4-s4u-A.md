# FORK-S4U — a union's pairwise judgement after declarations retire

## For Ev

**Recommendation (sure): the pairwise pass stays, and becomes the union's coincidence door.** Every member pair whose closed boxes meet is built as the two-member union, as today. What that build decides Zero is recorded as the union's coincidence rows, in member space, and is what the `unproven-coincidence` lint reads. What it refuses (the sliver band's `Escalated`, or anything else the pair verb refuses) refuses the union, in every member order. The fold builds the body and decides nothing the pairs did not: a fold step that decides a Zero no judged row backs is an emission bug (today's `fold_step_refusal`, widened from two refusal kinds to every Zero). The fold's own records are the result's contact records for the census (D1 (ii)), carried as today, and are not the lint's. The alternative, the fold alone recording what it meets, is laid out below and rejected: it makes findings and refusals depend on member order, which is the defect the pairwise rule was built to remove, and it reverses two of your rulings rather than one clause.

**Premise check.** The brief names two surviving purposes for the pass, order-free refusals and the records. There is a third, already ratified and not touched by stage 4: a union's face names are linked through the pairwise judgement (N2's union paragraph, your ruling on PR 3734: "`judge_pairwise_contact` must keep each pair's `BooleanNaming`; union linking must read those consumed pairs before the fold"). The links are what make a merged face's name the same in every member order. So "does a pairwise pass survive" is settled by naming unless that ruling is reversed too; the open question is its role, what it emits, and whether the fold's decisions are the record or a check against it. Terms: a *row* is stage 4's `Coincidence` record (two cells, a relation, the deciding site, the margin); *member space* means the cells are named `FromMember { member, of }`, the member's own face or edge, never a row the fold minted; *the fold* is the left fold of the pair verb in list order that builds the body.

**What D10 asks of a union.** D10 records "every coincidence the kernel infers from values" and the lint asks whether each holds structurally, that is across the family of values. A coincidence between two members is a fact about those two members' constructions. Whether a third member covers the contact, or whether the fold happened to join the two before the third arrived, says nothing about whether the coincidence is structural, and the family can move the third member off the contact as easily as it can move the two apart. So the set of coincidences the lint should see is the set over member pairs, and the fold's set, which depends on order, is a sample of it. This is the D10 form of your #3200 ruling: "a covered contact is not a contact" was rejected because it "lets a whole set avoid declaring a contact just because no single pair is blamed for it"; under D10 the same set must not avoid a finding because the member order hid it.

### The final state recommended

- **Judgement.** Pairs visited in one fixed order (today: ascending node id, lesser id as operand A). A pair whose closed boxes are disjoint is skipped; nothing else is (declared pairs, which were judged whatever their boxes, are gone). Each judged pair runs the pair verb as `m ∪ n`; its body is discarded; its rows and its naming record are kept.
- **The union's rows** are the judged pairs' rows, each cell mapped to its member's name through the member's table (the pair's emitter over the two member tables, as every op maps its rows in unit B), listed in a canonical order (pair, then the pair's own decision order). A row's existence and content depend on the members and never on the list order. A row whose cells the fold later consumes whole is still a row: it names member cells, which exist whatever the fold does.
- **Refusals.** A pair's `Escalated`, or any other refusal of the pair verb, is the union's refusal in every order, as DM4 already says ("even where a third member covers it"; PR 3213 measured a covered grey-band pair going from refusing in 2 of 6 orders to 6 of 6). A sliver between two members is a sliver of the model at this ε, whichever member hides it.
- **The fold.** It glues on Zero like any boolean (unit E), and every Zero it decides must be between cells that descend from member cells some judged row relates; otherwise it is an emission bug, which is what `fold_step_refusal` asserts today for the two contact refusals. Its records are the result's `ContactRecords`, the census's (D1 (ii), (iii)); they back onto the union's rows and are not a second lint input. A three-way coincidence (a vertex the fold minted at `a ∩ b` lying on `c`'s face) is backed by the pair rows `(a, c)` and `(b, c)` at that point, so the assertion holds for it too.
- **Links from rows (likely).** N2's links are today read off `BooleanNaming::merge_groups` and `covered`. Both are same-oriented coplanar face pairs whose regions meet, which is exactly a `SameOriented` face–face row from the same pair build; the links are the transitive closure of those rows. One record, two readers (the lint and the namer), and `judge_pairwise_contact` no longer keeps a `BooleanNaming` per pair. If a `covered` pair exists that no row states, the row emission is incomplete and that is the defect to fix, not a reason for two sources.
- **What it makes representable.** A finding on a contact no order of the fold meets: yes, and intended. A finding that differs by member order: unrepresentable, by construction. Records for cells that do not survive in the result: yes, in member space, where every cell exists.
- **Cost.** Unchanged from today: the pair verb over every box-overlapping pair, plus the fold. PR 3213 measured the die (21 members, pips pruned) at 0.111 s against 0.115 s before the pass, and twelve mutually overlapping blocks at 0.461 s against 0.143 s. Stage 4 adds naming each pair's rows, which is small beside a build. DM4's sentence stands: a measured problem is raised, not optimised around. The one real cost is structural, not time: the union is only as capable as every pair union, so a pair the kernel cannot build refuses the union even in orders where the fold never joins the two directly. That is today's ratified state and I would keep it (a pair that cannot be built cannot be judged), and it is reversible by the contact-only door below.
- **Reversible.** Yes: the fold already records and refuses on its own under unit E, so dropping the pass later is a deletion plus a new source for the links.

### Rejected: the fold alone

The fold glues on Zero, records what it meets, and the pass is deleted. Pros: n − 1 builds instead of up to n(n−1)/2; no pair union has to build on its own; what is recorded is literally what the body glued. Cons, each decisive: the covered contact is recorded in the orders where the fold meets it and not in the others, so the lint's findings are a function of a list the user may reorder for any reason; a covered sliver refuses in some orders and builds in others, the defect `union-refuses-in-some-member-orders-and-publishes-in-others` was opened on; and N2's links need a new, order-free source, which is what the pairwise judgement was ruled to be. It changes DM4's rule and the PR 3734 ruling, and D10 does not ask for either change.

### Not a mechanism: a contact-only door

A kernel door that decides the two members' Zero verdicts and margins without building the body would make each pair cheaper and lift the "every pair must build" constraint. But the Zero verdicts, the sliver escalations and the covered/merged facts are decided inside the boolean's classification and output stage, so a second door would be a second statement of those verdicts, kept in step by hand. If the pass is ever measured too slow, this is the cache to weigh then, with the pair build as its oracle; nothing in stage 4 should wait on it.
