# FORK-S3P — what a free pose is, and what a space's member is (designer A)

## For Ev

**Recommendation (likely): a free pose is a `Frame` with no value — it *is* a space's origin and axes — and nothing is ever computed "in the frame of a space": a construction computes in the free frame it is written on, a copy's pose is relative to what its bundle reads, and every operation over copies computes in the construction frame of the first copy it reads. The "earliest member" rule goes.**

### Premise check

- The brief asks what value a free pose has. The question beneath it is that D10 wrote one `Free` shape (value, unit, distribution) for every kind, and a frame is a torsor: it has no canonical point to be a value *at*. A scalar is free *at* 5 mm because scalars have an origin and a unit; a frame is free by being the thing others are measured from. So "free pose" is not an empty category to be worked around (the spec's valueless `Seed` definition): it is the one place a free pose exists, under its own name.
- The brief's second half assumes a space *has* a computing frame to pick a member for. It need not. A space is a set of related things; a frame only ever belongs to a member, and each thing the kernel computes can pick its own, from what it reads. That dissolves "what happens when two spaces merge".
- D10's "so an unrelated edit moves no bit (D9)" cites D9 for a promise D9 does not make. D9 says same inputs → same bits; locality under edits is D10's own, new promise. It should be stated where it lives, as a property of each operation (below), and D9 cited only for determinism.
- Provenance: "frame of its earliest member" and "a part is born in its own space" were agent-written in the D10 draft (`84404cdbf`) and ratified in passing; your own words on it were "i would've expected there to still be some freedom in the choice of those coordinates. but it is fine to have none of those choices depend on the world node". So the earliest-member rule is a guess at your principle, and I propose replacing it with the principle. `VarDef`'s "may be free or defined" for poses was written in #4222 (FORK-1), also in passing.

### Part 1 — a free pose

**A free pose is a `Frame` and holds nothing: no coordinates, no unit, no distribution.** It is the origin and axes that every pose defined on it is measured from. Only `Frame` may be free: a free `Plane` would be a space known up to in-plane motion (its `Subgroup` is `Planar`, A11 (1)), and nothing can be built in a space whose symmetry is not `Trivial`. Every other pose, and every `Frame` that is not free, is defined: `InFrame { frame, coords }` (coordinates, as scalar variables, in a frame), `Offset { base, by }`, a projection, a named construction, `FaceFrame { face, spin }`. Numbers appear only there, so a tolerance on where a datum sits is a tolerance on those scalars (VR8 unchanged); a distribution on a free frame would move everything on it together and be observable by nothing, which is why it has none.

- **A document is born with one free frame**: its construction frame. A part's construction is written on it and has coordinates in no other. Today's absolute datums migrate to `InFrame { frame: <that frame>, coords }` over the same scalar variables, so every body digest is bit-equal (the spec's PR A already says this; only the name of the base changes, from `Seed` to the free arm).
- **A part may have any number.** Each further free frame (declared, or arriving by inline, VR9) is a further construction space. A definition whose reads bottom out in two free frames refuses at the door, by the same walk VR3's acyclicity uses: an axis on one frame and a point on another make no plane. A boolean of bodies on two free frames refuses (D10, Booleans, unchanged). Bodies on different free frames are related only by placements of their copies, never by a construction: that is "a part has no location" in its strongest form, since a construction is never moved.
- **The world is a free frame too**, the one the document marks undeletable and that no definition may read: only bundle mates and export read it. One representation, one role flag, instead of the spec's separate `World` arm; "construction never reads the world" is then a door check on reads of that one variable.
- **What D10's free-variable sentence says**: "a variable is free — for a scalar, a value, its written unit (D6) and optionally a distribution; for a `Frame`, nothing, since a free frame is what everything written on it is measured from — or defined …". `Point`, `Direction`, `Axis` and `Plane` are only defined.

*Alternatives.* (1b) The spec's shape: no pose is free, and a `Seed` definition over no variables plays the role. Same semantics, worse names: a definition over nothing contradicts D10's "defined by an `Expr` over other variables", and "may be free or defined" becomes false for every pose. (1c) A free pose holding coordinates in a document-level ambient frame: that is today's absolute datum, the location you retired; its tolerance would be observable only against the world. Rejected.

### Part 2 — what computes in what frame

**Rule: a frame belongs to a member, never to a space.**

- A **construction** (a body built from definitions on a free frame) computes in that free frame, which is the identity. There is nothing to choose.
- A **copy** (`Place { body, mates }`) has no coordinates of its own. Its pose is *relative to what its bundle reads*: the fold of its mates against one target space. The pose between any two members of a space is composed along the bundles between them — the path through the `Place` nodes, which is recipe structure — never through a root. So a relative pose is a function of exactly the mates and definitions on that path.
- An **operation over copies** (a boolean of copies, a cross-copy measure, each pair of the at-rest census) computes in the construction frame of the first copy it reads, mapping the others by their relative poses through `transform_rigid`. REFERENCES DM4 already judges a union's contacts "pairwise, in member space"; this is that rule made general.
- The **world** is a free frame nothing is built on, so nothing computes in it; export alone composes each world copy into it. A space whose copies read only the world is still computed copy by copy, in the copies' construction frames, so the world mate's numbers reach no body bit (your "depends on nothing about the world node").
- **Face-frame zero spin (DM1)** stays recipe-determined: a copy's carriers carry the construction's `u_ref` transported by the linear part (`topo/transform.rs`), so a sketch on a copy's face turns with the construction and not with any computing choice; a planar carrier an operation over copies mints (a chamfer of a boolean of copies) takes its reference in that operation's frame, which is the first operand's construction frame, fixed by the recipe.

**What this makes true that the earliest-member rule cannot.** With F's overconstraint rule, adding a mate never changes an existing relative pose (a pinned copy refuses, an unpinned one becomes pinned), and deleting one only un-pins. Since no operation's frame depends on the space, **no edit outside the path an operation reads moves a bit of it**: placing a floating subassembly into the main one, or re-mating the world, moves nothing already computed. Under earliest-member, that same edit re-roots the merged space and moves every cross-copy bit of whichever side was later in document order — the whole main assembly, if the subassembly's root was minted first — and "earliest" can disagree with "the member that reads nothing" after a bundle edit, re-rooting a space onto the copy that was just placed. Both are moves under a related edit, so they do not break D9, but they are moves the recipe did not ask for, and they are what your "freedom in the choice" was pointing at.

**Members, stated for the rule.** A space's members are its free frame (or the world, or a copy whose bundle is empty: the root, which reads nothing) and every copy reaching it through bundles. A bundle's mates all read one space, else it refuses typed ("place one of them against the other first"); so each space has one root and the question of which member's frame a space takes does not arise. Today's `Space::Own { group }` becomes "a copy whose bundle is empty is a root, and what reads it is its space"; `Space::World` becomes "a root whose bundle reads the world"; "its earliest instance at that frame's origin" becomes "the root is the identity of its own composition", which is what it already computes.

*Alternative (2b)*, the spec's: a space's members are its seeds and copies, and it computes in its earliest member's frame. What it alone makes true: one coordinate system per space, so a space-wide broadphase runs without per-pair mapping. Costs: the re-rooting moves above; a tie-break for several copies mated directly to the world; and `SolvedPoses` storing root-relative frames, so the representation itself names a root. Reversible either way; (2a)'s representation (a copy's pose relative to its target) can be evaluated root-relative later, the reverse not.

## Round 3

**1. Ev's principle bears on it, and the "rounding bits only" claim is false in two places.** (a) Carriers an operation mints take their u-reference from `Vec3::orthonormal_basis`, which *branches* on the normal's components against the coordinate axes (`|n.z| ≤ max(|n.x|,|n.y|)/2` picks `e_z` or `e_y`; the `FaceFrame` doc spells out "wall" versus "cap"). That is an absolute-orientation tie-break of exactly the kind D9's addendum names as a documented residual, so DM1's zero spin on a face minted by an operation over copies (a chamfer of a boolean of copies, a swept face) depends on the computing frame by a branch: a sketch there can turn by a quarter turn, not an ulp. (b) The kernel is not verified equivariant (D9, "do not cite the kernel as equivariant"), so a margin can cross into or out of the sliver band under a frame change; the band refuses rather than deciding, so the visible effect is a refusal appearing or vanishing, never a silent different glue. Names are keyed by recipe ids and do not move; topology moves only through (b). So the frame is an author-visible choice in two ways, and Ev's principle applies: it must be said, not hidden.

**2. The alternatives.**
- *Mint order* hides an order: creation order is invisible, not editable, and a delete-and-re-add changes it silently. It is the union's sort one level up, and withdrawn for the same reason.
- *The operation's stated operand order* is the author's visible statement. A pair boolean is already asymmetric in it: D1 keeps the first operand's description for a merged face, and the fold order is now the author's (S4U). Taking the first operand's construction frame adds no new arbitrariness; it reads the one the author already stated.
- *No choice at all* exists where every operand is a construction on one free frame, which is all of part modelling: the common free frame, and there is nothing to pick. It does not exist for an operation over copies of bodies from different free frames: no frame among them is distinguished without an order or a value. But the first-operand rule *contains* the no-choice case: when the operands share a free frame, the first operand's frame is that frame. So one rule covers both, and the only case where it states an order is the case where some order is unavoidable.
- *The census* has no author order and is a check, so it gets an order-free definition instead: each pair's verdict is a margined predicate symmetric in the pair up to the band, where it refuses. ### Round 4 addendum — the computing frame under Ev's principle

**The principle first.** The computing frame exists for two things: well-conditioned arithmetic near the geometry being built, and a memo that survives edits. The second has a precise form: the frame must be a function of what the operation reads and of nothing else, so it is keyed with the operation's inputs and an edit that leaves the reads alone recomputes nothing (D9). Per-space rules died on exactly this: a space's root is not something a boolean reads, so an unrelated placement invalidated it. Per-operation rules all satisfy it; what distinguishes them is only conditioning.

**Is the first operand's construction frame good numerically?** It is good whenever the author drew the first operand near its own origin, which is the normal case and the same assumption today's part modelling already makes: for a boolean of copies, the region that matters is where the operands meet, which is near both of them, and so near the first one's origin at the distance the author put between the body and its origin. It is bad in three cases, none created by the rule: a body drawn far from its own origin (`a-far-meeting-point-fails-membership-by-its-own-rounding` is this shape, at 1e7 m); a copy placed far from the first operand in an operation that nevertheless reads both (a clearance across a 100 m assembly, where one operand's coordinates are large whatever frame is shared); and small features on large coordinates in general. A **recipe-derived** choice cannot do better: the recipe knows orders (operand, mint, root) and no distances, so no recipe rule is numerically informed. A **value-informed** choice can, and Ev's principle allows it: a frame derived from the operands' values, say the first operand's extent-ball centre snapped to a coarse exact grid so that small edits do not move it, is still a function of the reads, so it is cached with them and breaks nothing. It reads values, which the agent-drafted "never from values" forbade; that clause was protecting determinism and the memo, and a function of the reads keeps both. The analysis lanes take it from the nominal, as they take a pattern's count.

**Where the stated order survives: as the tie-break.** Constructions in one document's coordinates choose nothing. Among copies, the author's operand order is today's mechanism and remains the tie-break for any value-informed rule (equal extents, or no values to prefer). It is never the principle.

**The branch finding stands; the principle changes who fixes it.** A minted carrier's u-reference from `orthonormal_basis` branching on the computing axes, and a margin crossing the sliver band, both make an output depend on the frame beyond rounding. Ev's principle makes the frame a free implementation choice, which is only legitimate if the operation's *meaning* does not depend on it: the body up to the rigid map, its names, and every verdict outside the band must agree in any two frames. So the branch is a defect at the mint site, not of the frame rule: a minted reference direction must be a function of the operation's inputs (a transported operand reference, a sweep direction), never of the axes; that is the row the orchestrator names, `a-minted-reference-direction-follows-the-computing-axes`, which is not on main yet and should be filed with this. Sliver crossings are inherent to any frame, the band refuses rather than guesses, and better conditioning makes them rarer, which is the point of the principle. The invariant to test is the one above: compute in two frames and compare up to the map.

**D10 sentence, principle over mechanism.** "An operation computes in a frame that is a function of what it reads and of nothing else, chosen so its arithmetic is well conditioned near the geometry it builds; never the world's frame, and never anything it does not read, so the frame is keyed with its inputs and an edit that leaves an operation's reads alone moves none of its bits (D9). The frame is not part of the operation's meaning: the body up to that rigid map, its names and every verdict outside the sliver band are the same in any frame, and a minted reference direction is a function of the inputs, not of the axes. The mechanism today: a construction computes in the document's coordinates; an operation over copies computes in the construction coordinates of its first operand as the author lists it, the others mapped by the poses their bundles compose. A value-informed choice may replace that, with the author's order as its tie-break."
