# The boolean door and tier 3 (revised after reconciliation round 1)

## For Ev

**Recommendation (likely).** A public door that returns a body guarantees tier 3, or tier 3′ when the body carries contacts, and takes only such bodies as operands. Both halves live in the type that already exists for it, `topo::AtRestBody`, a body kept together with its tier-3 verdict: the boolean takes `&AtRestBody` operands and returns its result through the kept gate, then runs the census over its own contact records. Each body pays tier 3 once, at the door that built it. The "description gap" the gate's doc names closed long ago; nothing waits on it. The backstop's volume inequalities stay; its positivity arm retires once tier 3's own volume check takes over the arm's interval re-derivation, not before.

**Terms.** A *description* is an edge's intensional account of where its locus lies (D2): `Intersection` of two named surfaces, a *chart image* in one face's parameters, or a *scaffold*, the sketch-time stand-in for an edge whose surfaces do not exist yet. *Tier 3* (`validate_geometric`) is the geometric at-rest battery: carriers re-certified, planar residuals, dihedrals, the transience fence (no scaffold at rest), the per-solid positive-volume sign (check 7). *Tier 3′* (`validate_pseudomanifold`) is tier 3 plus the coincidence census checked both ways against declared contacts. A body's *currency* is the tier its wrapper claims: `BooleanBody` with contacts is 3′-grade, without is tier-3 (ratified under tier 3′, "validity class rides the result wrapper"). A *door* is a public construction entry; D1 rules that a door's check is a claim about the state it hands back, paid once at the door's end. The *backstop* is the door's volume-inequality check, which cannot see a short positive intersection.

**Premise check (sure).** The gap is not open. "PR 3 description gap" names M3 PR 3's state, where boolean and split results carried chord descriptions on seam edges, so tier 3 was reachable only through a test-side upgrade helper, and honest descriptions were "a PR 6 obligation" (the boolean acceptance suite's header still says so). M3 PR 6a delivered them: the door mints `Intersection` on every transverse seam edge at mint time (`describe_minted_edges`), and both acceptance suites now assert tier 3 directly on shipped results. The gate stops at tier 2 because a stale comment says so. What the measurement actually found is three unrelated things: test fixtures handed to the door below tier 3 (49 of 52), the door's own seam-description defect on slivers (3 of 52, filed), and the class the dual review hit, a closed body with an off-carrier edge, which only tier 3 sees. The real question is the door contract: what "at rest" means at a door. D1 says a body handed out is a plain value at rest, tier 3 is the at-rest validity, yet shell gates tier 3, the boolean, split and the sweeps gate tier 2 and defer to a "workspace convention" that is written down nowhere, and only the document layer's product gate and import actually re-validate. So a wrong mid-recipe result becomes the next verb's operand, and an operand with edges off its own surfaces ends a boolean on a kernel invariant rather than a typed refusal (`work/hone/a-stranded-operand-reaches-the-classification-invariant`).

**The final state.**
- `boolean_op_with(a: &AtRestBody<T>, b: &AtRestBody<T>, …)`. A body built directly with Euler operators reaches the boolean only through `AtRestBody::validate`. The precondition that classification and the surface intersection already assume, certified operand geometry, becomes a type rather than an unstated rule, and an operand below tier 3 is refused where it is gated, naming its own entities, instead of ending on an invariant inside the door.
- `gate` runs tier 3 through `T::gate_at_rest_kept` (tiers 1 and 2 are its first act, so the present double tier-1 run goes away), then `T::gate_at_rest_declared` over the result's contact records. Both go through `AtRestPolicy`, as the backstop does: present at `f64`, `Interval`, `Probe` and `Sym`, structurally absent at duals, whose outcome says so. `BooleanBody.body` is the `AtRestBody`; a consumer wanting a plain body calls `into_body` and gives the verdict up. A verdict cannot be carried to another body, so nothing is checked twice and nothing is claimed unchecked.
- Refusals are the validator's findings, typed. The arc-in-a-plane body of the dual review refuses at check 5 (`PlanarBoundaryResidual`); the three slivers refuse `ScaffoldAtRest` and `LaminaWedge` until the filed seam lever lands; the 4 short positive intersections the backstop cannot bound refuse at tier 3.
- The same contract for split, extrude, revolve, loft and blend: their doors return `AtRestBody` and shell's own tier-3 call becomes the shared gate. The evaluator carries `AtRestBody` between nodes, so the product gather, whose doc says tier 3 is a local battery, can keep the verdicts it is handed and pay only the census.
- Ratified text: this is a change to D1's tier-2 sentence, not an addition beside it. "Finished bodies must pass tier 2; tier-1-only states are visible solely inside operation sequences, never across an API boundary at rest" set the bar when tier 2 was the highest tier that existed (the M1 ratification sweep, while tier 3 was "named now, not implemented"); two sentences setting two bars for one boundary would state the same thing twice. Proposed: *A body a public door returns passes tier 3, or tier 3′ when it carries contacts, and a door takes only such bodies as operands (`AtRestBody`). States below that are visible solely inside operation sequences, never across an API boundary at rest.* Tier 2's definition in the tier list is untouched; a tier-2-only body is a closed solid without geometric certification, not Euler scaffolding, and the sentence should not call it that.
- The backstop. The inequalities check what tier 3 cannot, that the solid is plausibly this op's answer, and stay. The positivity arm (`encloses_material`) is tier 3's check 7 read at the door, with one difference that matters today: the arm re-derives a sum that reads negative in interval arithmetic, and check 7 does not. Check 7 calls the valid 6e-19 m³ sliver of `contact9_side_codes` `NegativeVolume` (`work/contact/volume-door-reads-a-tiny-valid-boolean-result-wrong`), which the arm reads right. So: move the arm's interval re-derivation into check 7, then retire the arm as a duplicate. Gating tier 3 at the door before check 7 has it would refuse that valid sliver.
- Reversible: a type on two parameters, a gate call and a wrapper field; dropping the operand type returns to gating the result alone. Cost does not change feasibility: tier 3 measured at about 15 % of op time on both corpora, paid once per body. The census at the door is unmeasured.

**Why not the alternatives.**
- *Gate the result at the door, operands as plain bodies.* My earlier position. It catches what the door mints, and avoids paying tier 3 three times per op only by trusting that every operand came through a door, which nothing enforces; a sub-tier-3 operand still ends on the stranded-operand invariant before the gate is reached, and when it does reach the gate the refusal names the result, the wrong party. The typed operand answers the cost objection outright, since a kept body is not re-gated, so this answer is now only a step toward the recommendation.
- *Leave the door at tiers 1–2 and rely on the product gate.* The kernel is API-first; the verb seat and the Python binding ship the body as it is, and a later union covering the bad edge can hide it inside a valid solid of the wrong shape.
- *Gate only the checks that see an off-carrier edge (3 and 5).* A fourth validity level nobody named, a second roster kept in step by hand, and it exempts the door's own scaffold-at-rest and lamina findings, which the transience fence exists to make loud.
- *Tier 3 at the door, census left to consumers (defensible, lean against).* Cheaper, and the local battery alone closes every measured case. Against it: a stale or undeclared contact on a boolean result is the door's defect, and the result's currency is 3′. If the census cost proves to be of the order of the op, this is the fallback, with the census still run at the assembly gate.

**Confidence.** Recommendation: likely. The gap closed: sure. The 49 are test-only operands: likely. The three slivers are the filed seam lever: sure. Check 7 misreads the valid sliver the arm passes: sure (the item and the test's own doc say so). Cost of tier 3: sure for the local battery, unsure for the census. That D1's sentence is not Ev's own wording: unverified by me.
