# The boolean door and tier 3

## For Ev

**Recommendation (likely).** The boolean door ships a result only after the at-rest gate of the currency it already claims: tier 3 when the result carries no contact records, tier 3′ over its own records when it does. The verdict rides the result wrapper, so an ungated result cannot be represented. Operands are not re-gated. The "description gap" the gate's doc names closed a long time ago; nothing waits on it.

**Terms.** A *description* is an edge's intensional account of where its locus lies (D2): `Intersection` of two named surfaces, a *chart image* in one face's parameters, or a *scaffold*, the sketch-time stand-in for an edge whose surfaces do not exist yet. *Tier 3* (`validate_geometric`) is the geometric at-rest battery: carriers re-certified, planar residuals, dihedrals, the transience fence (no scaffold at rest), the positive-volume sign. *Tier 3′* (`validate_pseudomanifold`) is tier 3 plus the coincidence census checked both ways against declared contacts. A body's *currency* is the tier its wrapper claims: `BooleanBody` with contacts is 3′-grade, without is tier-3 (ratified under tier 3′, "validity class rides the result wrapper"). A *door* is a public construction entry; D1 rules that a door's check is a claim about the state it hands back, paid once at the door's end. The *backstop* is the door's volume-inequality check, which cannot see a short positive intersection.

**Premise check.** The gap is not open. "PR 3 description gap" names M3 PR 3's state, where split and boolean results carried chord descriptions on seam edges, so tier 3 could only be reached through a test-side upgrade helper and the honest descriptions were "a PR 6 obligation" (the boolean acceptance suite's header still says so). M3 PR 6a delivered them: the door mints `Intersection` on every transverse seam edge at mint time (`describe_minted_edges`), and both acceptance suites now assert tier 3 directly on shipped results (`assert_tier3_posture`, and split's target-9 row marked RESOLVED). The gate's doc cites a posture that no longer exists. What the measurement actually found is three unrelated things: test fixtures handed to the door below tier 3 (49 of 52), the door's own seam-description defect on slivers (3 of 52, already filed), and the class the dual review hit, a closed body with an off-carrier edge, which only tier 3 sees. None of them is a decision about descriptions. The question that remains is whether the door enforces the currency its wrapper already declares. I think that is plainly yes; what needs you is the general rule and its DESIGN sentence.

**The final state.**
- `gate` runs tiers 1 and 2, then `T::gate_at_rest_kept` (tier 3, verdict kept as `AtRestBody`), then `T::gate_at_rest_declared` over the result's contact records (the census). Both go through `AtRestPolicy`, exactly as the backstop does: present at `f64`, `Interval`, `Probe` and `Sym`, structurally absent at duals, whose outcome says so. The backstop stays; it is cheap and bounds what tier 3 does not (∪ and ∖ against the operands).
- `BooleanBody.body` is an `AtRestBody<T>`, whose only constructor of `Validated` is the gate. A consumer wanting a plain body calls `into_body` and gives the verdict up. The verb seat's exhaustive destructure moves with it. This is the same two-step the document layer runs over a product and an assembly, so the shapes already exist.
- Refusals are the validator's own findings, typed, under `ResultInvalid`. The arc-in-a-plane body of the dual review refuses at check 5 (`PlanarBoundaryResidual`); the three slivers refuse `ScaffoldAtRest` and `LaminaWedge`; the 4 short positive intersections the backstop cannot bound refuse at tier 3.
- Operands are the responsibility of the door that shipped them. The boolean neither re-gates them at tier 3 nor exempts their defects: a result built from a sub-tier-3 operand ships iff it passes, else the refusal names the result entity. All 49 such results in the corpus come from hand-built fixtures that skip the description step and from two far-origin refusal rows; the tests take the step or pin the refusal, and no non-test code moves for them.
- A DESIGN sentence under the tiers, after "Finished bodies must pass tier 2": *A public door that ships a body at rest runs the at-rest gate of the currency its result claims, once, at the door's end, over the state the caller will see; the verdict rides the result.* Shell and import already do this; split, extrude, revolve and loft gate tier 2 only and become the rule's follow-on. The sentence binds future doors, so it is yours to ratify.
- Reversible: a gate call and a wrapper field. Cost does not change feasibility: tier 3 measured at about 15 % of op time on both corpora. The census at the door is unmeasured (below).

**Why not the alternatives.**
- *Leave the door at tiers 1–2 and rely on the document layer's product gate.* It does catch the arc body one door later with root attribution. But the kernel is API-first: the verb seat and the Python binding ship the body as it is, and a wrong body chains into the next op, where it refuses as a join desync or loose ends that the text calls a kernel defect. D1's "caught one door later" trade was for Euler operators inside one door's surgery, not for a public result crossing the API.
- *Gate only the checks that see an off-carrier edge (3 and 5).* A second roster of tier 3 kept in step by hand, and it exempts the door's own scaffold-at-rest and lamina findings, which the transience fence (your correction of 2026-08-27) exists to make loud.
- *Also gate operands at tier 3.* Pays tier 3 three times per op against D1's once-per-door rule, and the refusal it adds belongs to the operand's door. Today's sub-tier-3 operands are test-only.
- *Tier 3 at the door, census left to consumers (defensible, lean against).* Cheaper and the local battery alone closes every case measured. Against it: a stale or undeclared contact on a boolean result is the door's defect, and the result's currency is 3′, so the door would ship a claim it did not check. If the census cost proves to be of the order of the op, this is the fallback, with the census still run at the document's assembly gate.

**Confidence.** Recommendation: likely. That the gap is closed: sure (the acceptance rows assert tier 3 directly). That the 49 are test-only operands: likely (the fixture docs say the description step is the caller's; the measurement names only test modules). That the three slivers are the filed seam lever (`seam-description-reads-a-dihedral-at-the-seams-own-length`): sure. Cost of tier 3: sure for the local battery, unsure for the census.
