Background, with the terms defined. The mesher turns each curved face of a solid into triangles in one of two **lanes**. The **rectangle lane** (`mesh::curved`) takes a face whose domain is a rectangle of its chart `(u, v)`, bounded by iso lines. The **chart lane** (`mesh::trimmed`) takes any other curved face: it reads the face's boundary as a polygon in the chart, every loop, and triangulates inside it. Until this unit `tessellate::lane_of` chose the lane by three tests on the KINDS of the outer loop's carriers (an ellipse, spline or spiric; on a sphere a circle with a projected image; a ring), and sent everything else to the rectangle lane, which then asked two **doors** in `geom_brep::props` whether the face is a rectangle (`require_iso_rectangle`, the shape door, and `require_one_chart_branch`, the branch door) and refused if not. So a face bounded by iso lines alone that is no rectangle, such as a pipe wall whose hole crosses the seam (a "notch"), reached no lane. The unit makes the doors' answer the route: both pass, the rectangle lane; decided no rectangle, the chart lane; the door cannot say or the data contradicts itself, a typed refusal with the door's cause. It is read once per face per run. A face that meshes on main and is routed to the other lane would move its bytes or refuse, and replay is byte-identical by contract.
