**What it is.** A fillet band's corner patch is a torus face whose foot on the support plane is an arc, joined at each end to a cylinder fillet's straight foot, tangentially. The pair is the plane against the torus across the arc. The torus is presented by its parallel; the plane's cell at the arc's end is `Local` at every depth: `present_curved` reads the arc through the vertex and `graph_read` fails in both columns. As `u` over `v` the arc is no graph, because its `v` turns exactly at the vertex; as `v` over `u` the run is monotone and `graph_read` returns `None` in its corner block, after `apart` and `alone`, at the premise check on the vertex or at `leaves_graph`. 24 tests, every one a `band_*` or `blend_recourse_followability` fillet, three traced.

**What is not known, and is yours to find first.** Which clause of the corner block returns, and whether the cause is what the trace inferred: that the next edge leaves the vertex along the arc's tangent (the band's construction), so `leaves_graph`'s rate of the line off the continued arc is zero at the vertex and no first-order reading signs it. Probe it at the unit level with the traced cell's inputs. If the joint is not tangent, or the clause is the vertex premise, the unit is a different one: say so before building.

**What to do.** Give the plane's corner a reading where its two edges meet at first order: the line read as leaving the continued arc at second order (the arc curves away from its own tangent with curvature 1/r; the line does not), or the two edges read as one C¹ run through the vertex so that the cell is presented beside one curve and not at a corner. They are not equivalent: weigh them as final states and say which you took and why.
