# The unit: `embed/corner-pieces`

**Row:** `work/embed/` the row whose name ends `…is-not-strictly-convex-is-read-at-no-depth.md` (on `origin/embed/straight-corner`, with that lane's corrections; your design goes in its `## Owed`). Letter H. Review tier: dual, a concurrent pair, at your report.

**What is to be built, as designed by the straight-corner lane and accepted by the orchestrator in principle (2026-11-22).** A plane face's part of a cell holding two line edges at a vertex is presented as TWO convex pieces, cone(a, m) and cone(b, m), where a and b are the two edges' directions from the vertex and the ray m into the face is the sum of the two edges' toward-face normals (the bisector for a convex and a reflex corner alike, the common normal for a straight one; it vanishes only at a 0° or 360° spike, which refuses). Each piece is a pointed sector with its own zero (`Side` on its edge if the pair shares that edge, else `Point` at the vertex if shared, else `None`). `departure` certifies the face pair where EVERY piece pair has SOME separator, built per piece pair. Then convex, straight, straight only to rounding, and reflex are one reading: no lean is decided at the band, no line is held within ε, `On` keeps exact membership, and what is conceded is only what `Zero::Point` already concedes. The lean gate, the convex arm's quadrant, the run and `Zero::Through` go.

**Why it is believed.** Two closed pointed cones with no common ray are strictly separable through the apex; a plane face's sector at a two-line corner, split by a ray strictly inside it, is two pointed cones that cover it; so a partner whose own pieces share no ray with them is parted piece pair by piece pair, and a shared ray is a real overlap. The lane's probe (`probe_pieces_of_a_corner_each_part_alone` on `origin/embed/straight-corner-measure`, a1e26e093): exact straight 4 of 4; ±1e-13 off straight 4 of 4; reflex by 207° and 260° 2 of 4, the two that split being separable and missed by main's heading plane; a partner lying along the face splits exactly the piece pairs that share a ray.
