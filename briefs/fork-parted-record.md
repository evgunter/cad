**The problem.** A split (a body cut by one plane into its two sides) carries its operand's contact records onto its sides through its lineage, and each side is gated at tier 3′ against the records it holds (`[topology.records]`: a finished body holds the records of its own touches). A record whose two cells the split's plane puts on different sides is none of the carry's consumption cases, so the split refuses it `Parted` — a legal split refuses. The row `work/doctail/a-record-the-split-parts-has-no-consumption-rule.md` (read it on `origin/main`) has the history: an interim rule (`carry_contacts_across`, used only by the document's `wire_split`) retires a parted record when each cell has an image on the cut, and refuses otherwise; whether the carry gains a consumption case is an open question for Ev, never weighed by designers.

New evidence (HELDRECORDS PR 7481, `heldrecords/split-on`, not yet merged): the split now records its own coincident edges (a slit's two edges, a ring's), per Ev's ruling on PR 5967. Consequence: a later cut whose plane contains a recorded ring's two edges (`a_keel_wrapped_at_both_ends_splits_as_a_ring::a_later_cut_across_the_ring_builds_and_so_does_one_through_its_tip`, the cut at y = 2 upright along the ring) now refuses `Carry { Parted }` where it built 7.85 | 7.85 before; neither edge bounds a cut face. Every recorded slit or ring a later cut runs along reaches it. Also `name_invariance`'s plate pinch at y = 1 (the row's original witness).

What needs deciding: what a split owes a record whose cells it parts — what becomes of the touch the record states, on each side, and what each side's tier 3′ then reads — as a final state of the code (topo's carry, the README paragraph, the document door's interim). The question may be at another layer (what a record of a touch IN the cut plane means at all; whether the cut's own contact with the touch is itself a record the split should write).
