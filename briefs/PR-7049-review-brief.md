Background, with the terms defined. A curved face is meshed by one of two lanes. The **rectangle lane** (`mesh::curved`) takes a face whose domain is a rectangle of its chart `(u, v)` and triangulates it by a Delaunay triangulation of the chart. The **chart lane** (`mesh::trimmed`) takes every other curved face; on a cone it triangulates in the **development** (the cone unrolled flat about its apex). `tessellate::lane_of` decides the lane. A **ruled face** is a face on a cylinder or a cone: its chart lines `u = const` are straight lines of the surface, its **rulings**. A **split side** is a straight side of the face carried by two or more edges, so it has a vertex part-way along; booleans make these. On the base, the rectangle lane kept such a face and laid a **fallback lattice** for it (`curved::grid_counts`, when `bare_sides` was false). On a cone through its apex that lattice put an apex copy and two nodes of one column on one ruling: a triangle of no area, which PR 7043's new rule refuses (`UnresolvedWinding`: every triangle's winding must be resolved above rounding). A cylinder wall with a split side refused `CertificateExceeded` (the per-triangle certificate: an upper bound on a flat triangle's distance from the surface, compared with the tolerance δ). The unit routes every such face to the chart lane, deletes the fallback lattice, and makes the rectangle lane refuse typed if one still reaches it. It is an INTERIM: the chart lane lays lattice rows these faces do not need and sizes at half of δ (`UNPROVED_HEADROOM`), so they come out denser than they need to be; that price is known and is a later unit's.
