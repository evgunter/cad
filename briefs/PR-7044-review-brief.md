Background, with the terms defined. Every curved face is meshed into flat triangles, and each triangle gets a **certificate**: an upper bound on how far the flat triangle is from the surface, compared with the tolerance δ. For a cylinder of radius `r` it is `cert::cert_cylinder`, `r` less the distance from the axis LINE to the triangle (`dist_line_triangle`, which projects along the axis and asks `dist_point_triangle` about the triangle's shadow), and for a sphere `cert::cert_sphere`, `r` less the distance from the centre POINT to the triangle. A certificate that is too SMALL is the unsafe direction: it passes a triangle that is over δ. On main, a sound triangle with a side along a ruling has a shadow of no area; `dist_point_triangle` divided by that area in some corner orders and answered NaN, and `.max(0.0)` read the NaN as a certificate of 0. So the answer depended on the order the corners came in. The unit rewrites the distance to be total and order-free, and makes a non-finite input poison the two certificates. It claims that no mesh the kernel returns today is over δ because of the defect and that no committed byte moves.
