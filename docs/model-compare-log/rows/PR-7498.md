**The cause was found by measuring, and it was not where the row looked.** The row's title gave the symptom. The implementer's first stage showed the triangulation running in raw chart coordinates, where a torus band's lattice cell is far from square, so a Delaunay triangle beside the rim's chord points reached across cells. The cure is one spelling of the point the triangulation sees (`cdt::lattice_point`), in the lattice's metric.

**The MAJOR, by inspection with the mechanism demonstrated on a synthetic triangulation:** the first build handed the rectangle lane the chart-lane's metric, whose sphere arm is the chart's own stretch at mid latitude, written for another lane. The rectangle lane's sphere lattice is sized for a lemma stated in a plane where cells are near square; under the borrowed metric 132 of 343 sphere faces triangulated on cells more than 2.8 times off square. No body was found that it broke. The orchestrator shares this one: told at stage 2 that off-equator sphere bytes moved, it accepted them on a sampled deviation without asking which lemma the lattice was sized for.

The MINORs: the class of faces that moves was wider than the PR said (a spindle of the suite moves); the stated worst deviation was the corpus's, not a bound; two rows inert under both mutants. A count in that review was wrong and the reviewer withdrew it at the delta: it reported 25 cylinder patches moving, the implementer's differential found none, and the re-taken instrument agreed with the implementer (0 of 7,749 cylinder, 0 of 4,520 cone, 237 of 2,987 sphere, 8 of 25 spindle).

**Spec deviations.** Reported: the cause. Silent, by the review: one row of the brief (a swept annulus) was neither ruled in nor out in the PR.

**Fix passes.** Two, by the implementer's own agent, continued. The first split the metric in two (`cell_metric`, the lattice's realized aspect to the nearest power of two, read by the rectangle lane on every chart; `trimmed_metric`, the chart lane's). In it the implementer corrected the orchestrator a second time: the orchestrator had claimed a sphere's cells are within √2 of square in raw coordinates, so that no sphere byte need move, and proposed nothing further; the implementer measured 0.42 to 2.4 on its bodies (the delta later measured 0.032 to 2.64 over the suite) and offered a second rule to hold sphere bytes still. The orchestrator ruled one rule on every chart, which the delta attacked and upheld. The second pass, after the delta: a false doc sentence corrected by re-measurement, an inert row deleted, a shared test builder moved, a second false doc clause corrected, and a latent gap on main filed (the chart lane's sphere arm triangulates a lattice sized for one plane in another; no defect found on 90 polar caps).

One attribution of the implementer's was wrong and caught before the review: it re-pinned the viewer's pick tally to main's own red value and called the difference its change's. A base run showed which six rays were its own.

**A row the last pass could not write, said plainly.** The delta showed the new aspect row pins the helper and not the lane's call site. The implementer built a relation on the emitted mesh to see the call site, found it green under the mutant (no sphere face of the suite carries a boundary point off its lattice), dropped it rather than add a test seam, and named the byte golden that does catch the mutant at the row.

**Hosted CI:** on `12614f22f1`, the head with all the code, `test (kernel)` passed 12,838 of 12,838 at two ε and failed one test at ε 1e-12 that is main's and tracked; lint failed on a gate that is red on main itself. The closing head adds docs, tests and a merge of main; see the PR for its run.
