## For Ev

**Recommendation (likely).** At the symbolic box scalar, a decision should read its margin from the *expression* as well as from the *value*, for every margin and not only for identities. Concretely: where the value's enclosure gives no answer, the tier encloses the margin's reduced form over the box's brackets, and the decision applies the ordinary band test to that enclosure (intersected with the value's). A zero form is then just the case where that enclosure is the single point 0. E12's sentence "Everything else decides numerically as today" is what stands in the way, and I would change it. The defect starts at what E12 lets the expression be used for, not at any reader.

Terms. *Margin*: the number a decision takes the sign of. *Value*: the interval computed op by op during the replay; it treats two occurrences of one parameter as two unrelated numbers. *Expression*: the graph of operations recorded beside the value, in which a parameter is one symbol. *Reduced form*: the expression as a polynomial quotient with exact coefficients, after like terms cancel; roots, `abs`, `min`, `max` stay as named sub-terms ("atoms"). *Band*: the tolerance interval about zero inside which a margin reads Zero. *Member*: one parameter point of the box.

**Premise check.**
- The brief says the tier "has no answer" for a form that is not zero. True of the ladder, but the tier already holds the needed operation. `sym::signed` encloses a reduced form over the parameters' brackets (`enclose_form_deep`, entering `sqrt`/`abs`/`min`/`max` atoms) and certifies a sign from it. Today it is used only *inside* a zero proof, to pick the arm of a `select`. So the tier can certify "this form is negative over the whole box" for an inner node and cannot say it for the margin itself. (sure: read in the code)
- The brief frames three kinds of margin. They need one answer, not three. A rounding-size constant, a definite function, and a coincidence that holds across the box are all "a real function of the parameters whose range over the box the value over-states". The right answer for each is whatever the band test says of the true range: Zero in band (unproven, exactly what a box of no width reads today) for the 6.6e-19 constant; the sign for `0.912·|top|`; undecided, then bisected and priced as a flip, only where the true range really crosses the band. (likely)

**The answers as final states.**

1. **The decision reads the form's enclosure (recommended).**
   - Makes true: a margin whose parameter cancels, or whose form has no zero in the box, decides at any box width at which the form's own enclosure is definite. One mechanism covers the identity, the rounding constant and the definite function. The value and the expression stop being two descriptions of one number reconciled only at zero.
   - Worked example (the tilted cap): value ± 0.094; form `6.58e-19`, no parameter in it; its enclosure is a sliver around 6.58e-19, inside the band; the decision reads Zero, unproven, as it does at every member. The follow-on vertex contact is then never asked.
   - Soundness: both enclosures contain the same real number at every member, so their intersection does. It holds on the same condition E12 already states for a symbolic Zero. The answer is about the real margin, not about each member's rounded `f64` margin; E12's Zero already has that property, and DESIGN `[simple.conditioned]` treats `f64` values as reals. (likely)
   - Leaves possible: a margin stays undecided where the form declines (a frozen form, a hull, an opaque or free value, a `select` or trig atom) or where the reduced form has its own repeated-parameter looseness (an expanded square such as `top² − 0.9·top + 0.2025` encloses worse than `(top − 0.45)²`). It is never worse than today, because the value's enclosure is still read.
   - Cost: one reduced form per undecided margin. That is the form the ladder already builds first, but PR 7712 exists to *avoid* building forms for margins that are not identities, so the two must be ordered and the cost counted (below).
   - Reversible: yes; it adds one reading at one site (`Sym::decide_sign`) and removes none.

2. **Keep E12; re-spell each reading so the parameter enters once (today).** Each re-spelling is local and exact. But the class recurs at every new reader, each has cost a unit, and the 15 November sweep of the census alone lists some thirty sites of this shape. Some re-spellings remain right for their own reason (one fact stored in two forms, such as an edge's direction as a line and as two ends) and should go on regardless of this decision.

3. **Carry first-order dependence in the lane scalar (the affine form E12 rejected).** It would also tighten values that are not decisions (a built point, a reported measure). But every operation pays for it, it is loose to second order, and it is a second mechanism for a fact the expression already holds exactly. I would not take it unless the count shows the loose values that matter are not decisions.

4. **Act at the body: make a vertex lie on its plane as an identity.** This addresses only the rounding-constant kind, and DESIGN `[intent.stored]` already says vertices are certified against a stated surface, never the surface fitted to them, so "on the plane to rounding" is a legitimate state whose right answer is "in band". Not an alternative to 1.

**What a count must show, and how to take it.** At `Sym::decide_sign`, under the existing test-only feature, for every decision the value leaves undecided over a box of non-zero width: (a) the `f64` answers at a handful of members (do they agree?); (b) whether the reduced form's enclosure over the brackets decides, and as what; (c) if it declines, why (frozen, hull, opaque, free, atom kind, depth); (d) if it is enclosable but still straddles while members agree; (e) the time to build the form. Run over the tour's first drive, M14's bodies at its four widths, and the pinned cases of the open rows. Answer 1 is right if (b) decides most of the member-agreeing margins and never contradicts (a), and wrong if most fall in (c), where the cause is values with no expression, or in (d), where a centred evaluation of the form is needed first. PR 7712's own count gives the population on the tour: about 22,600 undecided asks refuted as non-identities, 13,152 declined through a hull.

**Confidence.** Recommendation: likely. The mechanism exists in `sym::signed`: sure. It decides M15's four kinds: likely (forms read from the report, not run). It decides the tour's large margins at acceptable cost: unsure; that is what the count is for.
