## The problem

There are two: plain `Interval`, and `Sym<Interval>`, which carries each value's expression beside its enclosure so that a margin that is zero as an identity can be proven zero. Today one switch keeps a sweep leaf's body at tier 2 at both box scalars: the body is not put through the at-rest checks over the box. The program exists to retire that switch.

Measured with the switch's box arm off (the item has the detail; re-check what you rest on):
- The tour's chain study drives at plain `Interval`. A link turned by a joint's box of ±0.03 rad is then finished at `Interval`, and 24 of its 30 edges refuse at the edge certificate's endpoint check: the margin is the distance from the stored curve's start to its vertex, which reads `[0, 7.7e-4]`: zero below by construction, the box above. 48 planar incidence checks escalate beside them. The same body's `f64` witness finishes.
- At `Sym<Interval>` the same body passes those checks (the identity is proven) and stops later, on the volume.
- With the switch on, no body is finished at `Interval` anywhere in the tour, the chain certifies, and the command runs to its end.

**What needs deciding:** what a leaf at plain `Interval` IS, and so what the switch's retirement means for it.
