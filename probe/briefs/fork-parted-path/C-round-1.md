DESIGNER REPORT, round 1 (label C): a-parted-path-with-no-turn-is-reported-as-an-empty-flip-crossing

## For Ev

**Recommendation (likely): I have moved. The certificate should compare the build, not the record of how it was decided.** Once every predicate of a leaf is definite, the leaf **certifies** when it builds what the witness builds, and is refused **`FlipCrossing`** when it builds something else, naming the first node that builds otherwise, what differs there, and the decision that turned where the log shows one. No third fate exists among decided leaves, so nothing parted is bisected and no refusal can name nothing. The decision log stays on every run as the explanation and as a test instrument's subject. It is no longer the gate.

One change to that position as first put: "the build" should be compared as the thing E6 says is shared, the topology, addressed by names. Per node: its standing (built, failed, poisoned), and for each body it outputs, its cells by name and kind and which named cells bound which. Name tables alone leave open whether two builds with the same names can be joined differently; comparing incidence under the names closes that question by construction instead of by argument.

**Terms.** *Witness*: the nominal run at `f64`. *Leaf*: a sub-box, replayed at the box scalar. *Verdict*: one logged decision (predicate, definite sign, role). *Choice*: a decision whose other sign can change what a node builds. *Check*: one whose other sign can only make it refuse. *Route*: a node's verdicts in order. *Turn*: one question, reached the same way, answered with the other sign. *Parting*: any other first difference of two routes. *Mark*: a note of which candidates a pruning query handed on. *Lane choice*: a reading that only picks which formula states a fact the body has either way (DESIGN `[verdicts.consumers]`).

**What moved me.**
- My first report said equal routes prove the same build and the converse is false, and then kept the route as the gate. That is the stand-in kept after showing it is one. I set the build gate aside as a large redesign, which is the cost of the change and not a property of the result.
- My stated reason against a narrower gate was wrong on its example. I said the side reader's rays are checks whose combined answer steers the build. They are, and the tree already records that answer once as a choice (`side_read`, through `k_stats::answered`, with the argument written at `topo::side_reader::SIDE`). The role stamps are few and deliberate: five `checking` scopes and two `Role::Check` call sites.
- A turned check that passes is real and is the witness's build (fact 1 below). My first report refused it as a flip "that bounds the certificate, not the cells". Every consumer of that class reads it as a change of the part: the range search draws a boundary there, and containment reads it as off-branch. A class that needs that caveat means two things.

**What the whole-record gate buys that a parity instrument does not: nothing that survives.** Its one gain was that, on a user's document outside the corpus, a mis-stamped role or a lane logging differently costs refused mass and never a false certificate. The build gate has that property more directly: a steering decision nobody logged still shows in what was built. The price of the whole-record gate was refusing leaves that are the witness's build and making a hand-kept logging ledger load-bearing for certification. I withdraw it.

**On comparing choices only** (the other report's gate). It is sound under one assumption (every branch that steers the build is a logged choice or a mark) and it settles the two check states. It is second because:
- a turned lane choice still refuses a leaf whose cells are the witness's (fact 3);
- a parting caused by geometry (a slide changes which boxes overlap, with the same cells) bisects to the floor and ends as budget mass, at up to the leaf budget's cost, for a leaf the build gate certifies in one replay;
- its "any other parting is a kernel defect, fail loud" cannot ship while deliberately unlogged picks exist, and E12 already rules that a question about the kernel is asked by a test instrument and not in a shipped build.
It is the right fallback if the build comparison cannot be made complete. In that fallback a parting is a third way a node leaves a leaf unsettled and bisects; nothing in the drive panics.

**What a consumer is told.** Certified: proven the witness's part. `flip_crossing`: proven another part over the whole leaf (every point of a decided leaf builds what the leaf builds), with the node and the difference named. Everything else: not decided. Containment and the range search's boundary then mean what they say. The kernel disagreeing with itself is no mass: a test replays each corpus document at a zero-width box on the witness and requires the whole log, checks included, to match; and a leaf holding the nominal that builds otherwise is a debug assertion in the one classifier.

**Facts checked** (all by reading; nothing was run).
1. *A check that passes with either definite sign exists* (sure). `geom_brep::Corner::convexity` decides `dihedral_sign` through a nonzero door and maps positive to convex and negative to concave; both pass. Tier 3 reads it inside a check scope (`topo::finish`, `checking(|| validate_closed(..))`), and `geom_brep::edge_wedge_over_as`'s doc says a constructor reads it as a check "the same for a crease of either turn". So "choices agree, one check has the other sign" can come from geometry: an edge that is convex at the nominal and concave over the leaf, with the same cells.
2. *Extra candidates do log decisions* (likely). In `topo::boolean::ops::walk_pairs` the box test is marked and "decides nothing", and a pair that passes it is then walked by the section certificate, whose decisions are logged; `resolve/vdiff.rs`'s slid-block passage describes exactly such a pair deciding a plane parallelism. `VerdictVector`'s doc sentence that such candidates "decide nothing about them" is wrong for this site. So a wide leaf can lengthen its route with no change of build.
3. *A lane choice is logged as a choice wherever it is decided outside a check scope* (likely). The role defaults to `Choice`, only `checking` changes it, and no door marks a lane choice; the audit ledger lists lane choices decided through the ordinary funnel (for one, `pcurve_cone_chart_ruling`: "Zero the closed form, in band or definite the general lane"). Not traced site by site, and no document was built that turns one.
