DESIGNER REPORT: a-parted-path-with-no-turn-is-reported-as-an-empty-flip-crossing

## For Ev

**Recommendation** (likely). A leaf whose replay is fully decided but whose record differs from the witness's is one of two things, and the driver should type them apart:

1. **Turned**: at the first place the two records differ, both runs asked the same question and got opposite definite answers. That holds over the whole leaf and over every sub-box of it, so the leaf is refused at once as `FlipCrossing`, and the evidence names the node, the predicate and the two signs. This is E6's case and stays as ratified. A turned *check* counts as well as a turned *choice*, with its role carried in the evidence.
2. **Parted**: the records differ and no question is shown to have been answered differently (another predicate at that place, one record longer, or a pruning query that handed on other candidates). Nothing is proven about such a leaf: not that it is the witness's build, and not that it is another. It is **unsettled**, the third kind beside "a decision was indeterminate" and "a node failed for a reason not proven definite", and it takes the path those already take: bisect on the variables the parting node reads; refuse `ReadsNoVariable` if it reads none; refuse `Budget` at the floor. It is never `FlipCrossing`.

The evidence is taken from the first difference of the very object the certificate compared, so "differs, and names nothing" cannot be constructed: `FlipEvidence::is_empty` and its doc go, and so do the range search's `FlipUnfounded` and the helper that re-derives this distinction downstream.

**Terms.** *Witness*: the nominal run at `f64`. *Leaf*: a sub-box, replayed at the box scalar. *Verdict vector*: per node, in order, every logged definite decision (predicate name, sign, role). *Choice*: a decision whose other sign can change what the node builds. *Check*: one whose other sign can only make the node refuse, or an instrument reading that is the same whichever way it reads. *Mark*: a note of which candidates a pruning query handed on; marks are kept for naming and are not in the vector.

**What a vector is, and what "the same vector" claims.** A vector records the ROUTE a build took: which questions were asked, in what order, with what answers. Equal vectors prove the same build, and that is all certification uses. The converse is false, and E6 assumes it: a different route is not a different build. Three sources of a different route with nothing turned, all reachable (sure for the first, by `resolve/vdiff.rs`'s own slid-block account and the measured defect; likely for the others, by reading):
- pruning hands on different candidates (a box-overlap test decides nothing but changes which pairs are asked about). At the box scalar boxes are fatter, so a wide leaf asks about pairs the witness pruned; a narrower leaf may not. Geometry and box width, no change of topology.
- the same, between two points of one topological region: a slide changes which boxes overlap. Geometry, permanent, and still the same cells.
- the kernel disagreeing with itself: one scalar logs a pass the other does not run (the measured fifteen premise checks). Never geometry.

**The three states of the item.**
- *Choices name different predicates at one index*: parted. From geometry (pruning) or from an unlogged branch; unsettled.
- *Choices agree end to end, check sequences differ*: parted. From geometry only through pruning inside a check (rays meeting other candidates); otherwise the kernel disagreeing with itself. Unsettled.
- *Choices agree, one check has the other definite sign*: this is a turn, not a parting. It can arise from geometry (an edge's convexity read at rest passes with either sign). It is decided over the whole leaf, so bisecting cannot change it: refused `FlipCrossing`, named. Today it is the one decided difference the evidence cannot name, because the driver borrowed the resolver's reading (which skips checks, rightly, since a check never causes a name to vanish) for a different question (where do the two records first differ).

**Why bisect, not refuse at once** (likely; one measurement could reverse it). Each terminal refusal the driver has rests on a proof that splitting cannot help: a sliver's enclosure is inside the band, a turned sign holds on every sub-box, a node that reads no variable sees the same inputs. A parting has no such proof, and in the fat-box case splitting is exactly what helps; it also lets a real turn hidden behind a fat-box parting be named at a narrower leaf. The price: a parting that is permanent bisects to the floor and spends leaf budget (default 65,536 leaves) that today one replay spends. The alternative final state, a terminal `Parted` refusal with its own mass class, is cheap and equally honest, forgoes whatever a narrower box would certify, and differs by one arm, so either is easy to reverse into the other. Deciding measurement: over the tour and the corpus, how many parted leaves have a half that certifies.

**What a refusal names.** Turned: node, predicate, role, from, to. Unsettled by parting: node, the index, what each run recorded there (a predicate and sign, or the end of its record), and the pruning query where a mark shows it. A `Budget` refusal for depth or grid should carry the node and the unsettled kind it stopped on; today it drops them, for all three kinds.

**What the masses mean after.** *Certified*: proven the witness's route, hence its build. *`flip_crossing`*: a recorded decision proven to go the other way over the whole leaf; the bound of the certificate, not a proof the cells differ. *`budget` / `reads_no_variable`*: not proven, now including partings. No new class. Containment becomes true only on decided differences.

**Considered and not recommended: compare choices only.** It would certify the second state outright and would have certified the plate through the premise defect, and it would retire the hand-kept rule that both scalars log the same checks. But it rests the certificate on every `checking` stamp being right: a wrong stamp today costs mass, and would then cost a false certificate (unsure how many stamps would survive an audit; the side reader's rays are checks whose combined answer does steer the build).

**The kernel disagreeing with itself.** That is a question about the kernel, never about a document, so by DESIGN `[simple.checks]` and E12's two-channels paragraph it is a test's to ask: for each corpus document, the box-scalar replay of the degenerate box at the nominal has the witness's vector or is undecided. One row was red for the defect; this makes every curved corpus document red for the next one. In the classifier, a turn on a leaf that holds the nominal is a defect: a debug assertion, and unsettled in a release build (the range search does this today; it moves into the one classifier).

**Text for E6**, replacing the no-flips bullet's first sentence, and nothing else: "a leaf on which a recorded decision is definite with the other sign than the witness's is refused mass (`FlipCrossing`, the decision named). A leaf whose record differs with no decision turned is not proven either way and is bisected like an indeterminate one."
