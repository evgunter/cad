## For Ev

**Recommendation (likely).** Over a parameter box, check 7 should read a body's side at **one member** of the box (one parameter point, with every value of the body worked out at that point), and let the **embedding certificate** that the same finish already proves over the whole box carry that side to every member. Today it tries to read the side with box arithmetic. A family of embedded closed surfaces that changes continuously cannot turn inside out, so the side is one discrete fact of the family. It does not need to be measured over the box. It is read once, and the finish's other box-wide proof says it holds for every member. The three items then stop being blockers for the switch's deletion: no ray is ever posed over the box, so no reading carries the box twice.

Terms. *Member*: the body at one point of the parameter box. *Box arithmetic*: `Interval` or `Sym<Interval>` values that enclose every member at once. *Side*: which side of a solid's boundary is material (check 7, the orientation invariant). *Embedding certificate*: tier 3′'s injectivity question, which the finish asks of each solid at every certifying scalar, box scalars included. It proves that no member's boundary meets itself except along its own edges. *Aimed ray*: the reader's line through a point on a face, from outside the body.

**Premise check (sure).** The brief, and the three items, treat this as a precision problem: readings that are one number at every member come out wide over the box. That is accurate, but it is a symptom. The question underneath is wrong. Check 7 asks box arithmetic for a fact that cannot vary across the family. Every wide reading the items list is a difference between two stored values that carry the same moving quantity, and an interval cannot see that they are one number (E12's own diagnosis). Re-posing such readings one at a time is the "per-site re-association" E12 rejected. The previous unit took the two readings that were identities, and what is left cannot be cancelled by arithmetic. So the three items are **one defect, and it sits in the question**. It is not in the reader's arithmetic, not in the body's stored description (storing incidences jointly would be a second description of one fact), and not in the scalar (interval arithmetic is honest, and `Sym` proves zeros by design).

**Why it is sound (sure on the mathematics; likely on premise 1).**
1. The members of a replay leaf are one continuous family. The leaf takes the same decisions everywhere, so its stored values are continuous in the parameters, apart from choices that change how something is written but not where its points are.
2. The finish certifies every member embedded, over the box (tier 3′).
3. An embedded closed surface encloses a region of positive volume. So its signed volume is continuous over the box and never zero, and its sign cannot change on a connected box. The side read at one member is therefore every member's side.

A family that does turn inside out inside the box has a degenerate member, and the embedding question refuses that member. A worked case: a tetrahedron whose apex passes through its base as a parameter moves. The member at the crossing is flat, tier 3′ fails there, and the finish refuses. That is today's answer, reached by a reason that holds at any width.

**What each part becomes.**
- *A ray* is what it is at `f64`: scratch, a line through an aim on a member's face. Over a box, no ray is posed on the family.
- *What the reader may know:* the member's stored values, plus the gate's box-wide embedding proof. It needs no incidence the walk does not hold: item 1's fork is answered by neither arm, because a member's readings are as narrow as `f64`'s. It does not need to know what built the body.
- *At `Sym<Interval>`* the member is the box's midpoint, each stored value re-valued from its expression at that point, at `Interval`. A value with no expression (an iterated root) keeps its box enclosure, and that reading widens honestly there.
- *At plain `Interval`* there is no expression to re-value. The body is a member only when its box is a point; over a wider box the reader stays as it is today, readable only over narrow boxes. Plain `Interval` is not the parameter-aware lane; E12 made `Sym` that lane.
- *At `f64`* nothing changes. The body is its own member, aims land where they land today, and the ground of the tie rows (the old SIDETIES, now merged into HONEJOIN) is untouched.
- *A body whose every face moves* is owed what every body is owed: its side at any width its embedding certifies. The tilted cap, the walls and the moving caps all read at the member.
- *The volume fallback* is no longer the same question. At the member it is tight. Over the box it stays a measurement question for whoever wants the number (`check-7s-volume-reading-is-loose-over-a-box`), off the deletion's path.

Consequence: check 7's reach over a box becomes exactly the embedding question's reach. The finish needs that proof anyway, so check 7 adds no limit of its own. Reversible: yes. The box-arithmetic reader is still there for plain `Interval`.

**Alternatives weighed.**
- *A ray that does not move:* the aim and direction become constants taken from the nominal values (a legitimate choice under `[scalars]`), so each reading pairs a constant ray with one stored entity. It is sound member by member without the continuity premise, and it also settles the seam-frame item. But it leaves the turn of a moving face's loops about the crossing point carrying the box twice. On the tilted cap I estimate it reads to about ±0.1, not ±0.25 (unsure, not measured). On the walls it reads only as far as the wall's top edge moving past a fixed aim (±0.19 on the slab). Second choice. Build it only if premise 1 fails.
- *`Sym` giving a value to forms that are constant in the parameters* (both wall and tilt items propose it). This changes E12's "everything else decides numerically". It does not reach the loops' turn, which is a sum of `atan2` terms whose constant total of 2π is a fact about winding, not algebra, so the tilted cap stays wide (likely). Not recommended for this.
- *Reading the aimed face's advance from its first edge's incidence* (item 1's first arm): this patches one reading, which the reading at a member makes moot.

**Ratified text.** Nothing in it blocks this. `[scalars]` already describes the pattern: "a discrete selection … checked at the lane scalar to stay the same over the box". Here the selection is the side, and the check is the embedding certificate. One short addition to `[topology.tiers]`, after "read by the one reader of a shell's role", so the gate's reasoning is stated: *"Over a parameter box it is read at one member and holds for every member by the embedding certificate: a continuous family of embedded closed surfaces does not turn inside out."* If premise 1 (a leaf is one continuous family) is not already stated in E6, it should be stated once there. That is what this rests on, and it is the driver's promise, not the reader's.
