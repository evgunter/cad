## For Ev

**Recommendation (likely).** Over a box, the side reader casts **one ray for the whole family**: a fixed line whose numbers depend on none of the document's variables, chosen as it is chosen today but read off the middle of the aim's enclosure. Every member of the family is then read against that same line. Today the ray is rebuilt from the body's own moving values, so it is a family of rays that rides with the body, and that is the single defect behind all three items. No ratified text changes: `[scalars]` already says a continuous value that depends on none of the document's variables is a choice and not a claim, and `geom-core`'s DL5.2 already says "a place the code chose to evaluate at ... is never a `T`". An aimed ray is such a place. The reader today builds it at the lane scalar as if it were a claim.

Terms. *Box*: a toleranced parameter's range. *Member*: the body at one value in the box. *Aimed ray*: the line the reader casts at a face from outside the body; the side is the sign of the first crossing it counts. *Advance*: distance along the ray to a crossing. *Turn*: how far a face's boundary winds about a point (2π inside, 0 outside). *Set aside*: a ray that read nothing; the next face is tried, then the volume's sign.

**Why the readings come out wide, and whose defect it is.** A finished body states a vertex, an edge's line and a face's plane as separate stored values (by design; the gate's certificates keep them in step). The reader builds its aim from one of them (the first edge's line) and then measures the others against it: aim against plane, aim against another edge, aim against a vertex. Each is a difference of two stored values that carry the same moving height, and an enclosure does not record that the two heights are one number (ERROR-DESIGN E12's own opening). So `h − h` reads as wide as the box. It is the reader's defect: it made the ray a function of the document's variables when nothing consumes the ray ("the ray is scratch", its own comment) and any ray from outside reads the same side. It is not the body's description, not check 7's question, and only at one residual (below) the scalar's.

**One defect, not three.** The aimed face's advance, the wall's aim hanging from its moving edge, and the tilted cap's in-plane part are the same subtraction at three sites. Two more open rows are the same defect and close with it: the curved carriers' `aim − origin` readings, and the aimed ray's direction read on a selected frame (at `f64` the frame is selected once, for one line).

**The final state.**
1. *The ray is a place.* Its aim and direction are plain numbers; its type says so, so a ray built from boxed values cannot be written (`[unrepresentable]`). One sentence per scalar: at `f64`, the ray it is today, bit for bit; at `Interval` and `Sym<Interval>`, one line for every member, and each reading encloses every member's reading of that line; at a dual, the base value's line, with no tangent.
2. *Each reading takes one stored thing against the line.* A plane's advance reads the plane's origin and normal against the exact line. Its width is then the truth: a cap that moves 0.25 really is met 0.25 earlier or later.
3. *Whether the ray crosses a planar face is the turn of the face's loops seen along the ray* (the loop's vertices dropped onto the plane square to the ray, about the ray's own point, which is exact). No crossing point is formed, so no vertex is subtracted from a point that carries the same height. This is what removes the tilted cap's in-plane part: each vertex's enclosure enters once.
4. *A crossing that lies definitely behind the first counted one stops nothing.* The side is the first count's sign; an exit whose face differs from member to member is not a reason to set the ray aside. (Separable from 1–3; without it the reach is smaller.)
5. What sets a ray aside is otherwise unchanged: a gap or an in-face reading that is undecided where it could be first.

**Worked example.** Slab of depth `1 ± 0.25`, ray aimed at the top cap. Today: the aim's height is `[0.75, 1.25]` (from the edge), the plane's is `[0.75, 1.25]`, and the cap's own advance reads `±0.51` where every member's is 0; the ray survives only by per-reading repairs. Proposed: the line passes through height 1.0. The member of depth 0.75 meets it 0.25 lower, the member of depth 1.25 0.25 higher: the advance `±0.255` is true. The bottom cap is met at 1.015, at least 0.76 later for every member. The cap's corners, seen along the ray, move 0.04 against an aim 0.19 from the nearest edge. The ray reads, and keeps reading until the box holds a member whose caps nearly meet. The bottom cap does not move at all, so its ray reads at any width. A wall's ray aimed 0.19 under the nominal top edge reads to about `± 0.19` and no further, correctly: beyond that some members' walls end below the aim, and the ray truly meets different faces at different members.

**What a body whose every face moves is owed.** A reading as wide as its members' real differences allow, and no wider reading than that from this reader. With a fixed line it reads while the box is narrower than the clearance of the aim and the gap to the next face; past that, bisection is narrowing a real width. One case remains a difference of two stored values: two faces that move together and stand closer than they move (a thin plate carried whole by a toleranced offset). The order of their two crossings is a single margin, a polynomial in the parameter, constant in it. That is the symbolic scalar's to value, at one decision site, and is the open row "a margin whose form is a nonzero constant escalates over a box". It does not stand before the switch's deletion: no measured body is of that kind.

**At `f64`.** Aims, rays, advances and edge gaps are unchanged bit for bit (the middle of a point is the point). The in-face turn's arithmetic changes (along the ray, not across the normal); it decides `±π` by construction and is not a tie site. One rule at every scalar; the tie census over the corpus is the check that nothing moved.

**What the reader may know.** The stored values alone. Not what the finish certified: the same reader reads shell roles inside Booleans on bodies no gate has passed, and its own in-face door says an admitted operand may hold a vertex off its plane by more than the band. Not what built the body. So the first item's fork ("may the walk take the first edge as lying on its face's plane?") is not answered either way: the question stops arising.

**Alternatives.**
- *Keep the moving ray; have the symbolic scalar value forms* (unsure it reaches). General in principle, and the volume needs it anyway. But the reader's wide values are not margins at decision sites: they are the aim, a crossing point, the arguments of `atan2`, a normalized direction. Valuing those means valuing every intermediate, at unknown cost, and plain `Interval` gains nothing.
- *Let the reader lean on incidence and on face charts* (the items' own proposals). Measured to help (walls `± 0.05 → ± 0.09`), but it is a repair per reading, each needing a premise the reader's other callers do not have, and a wall's aim still hangs from a moving edge.
- *Read the side at one member and let the gate's embedding certificate carry it across the box.* Mathematically sound (the side cannot change across a box every member of which is embedded; the brick rows' own header says so) and width-free. But the gate holds no member, only the driver does; it takes a verdict from a point, which `[scalars]` reserves to the lane scalar; and Booleans' shell roles have no such certificate. It can be added later without undoing the recommendation.

**The volume reading** is the same fact read by another instrument, and a different defect: it casts no ray, and its width is the scalar's (its sums subtract stored values; its form is the depth itself). It stays the fallback for bodies no ray can cross.

**Confidence.** The ray is a choice under `[scalars]` and a place under DL5.2: sure. The three items are one defect: sure. A fixed ray's readings are sound for every member: sure. The slab, the tilted brick and the walls read as described: likely; nothing was built or measured here. The tracker holds one supporting measurement (2026-11-14, hand substitution: "a ray constant across the box" certified the ± 0.05 slab in two leaves at 55–61 ms against four at 205–220 ms). Item 4: likely. Proposed design-doc text: none.
