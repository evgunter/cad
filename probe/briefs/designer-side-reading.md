## The problem
Tier 3's check 7 decides which side of a finished body's boundary is material. It reads the side by rays aimed at the body's faces and, where no ray reads, falls to the sign of the body's volume. At `f64` this decides.

Over a parameter box (a body one of whose dimensions is a toleranced parameter, at `Interval` or `Sym<Interval>`), a body is a family of members and the reading has to hold for all of them. On the bodies measured, every member gives the same answers: the advance of an aimed ray to its own face is 0 at every member, the turn of a loop about a crossing is 2π at every member, a ray's gap from an edge is one number at every member. Over the box those readings come out as wide as the box or wider, and the ray is set aside:

- On the extrude door's unit slab of depth `1 ± w`: the caps' rays read to `w = 0.25` and `0.46`; the four walls' rays to `w = 0.05`. On a brick whose top is tilted, one aim of six reads at `w = 0.05` and none from `0.1`. A body none of whose faces is square to the direction it moves in reads by no ray over a wide box.
- The volume it then falls to reads a slab's sign only below width `0.03` (its own row).
- The lane that last worked here re-posed the readings that were arithmetic identities (the loops' turn across a face's normal, the aim's step) and measured what is left: every remaining wide reading is a difference between two separately stored values that carry the same moving quantity (an aim built from one edge's stored line against a plane's stored origin, against another edge's stored line, against a vertex), or a moving quantity that lies in the plane a reading is taken in. The three items say which reading stops which ray, with tables.

These three items stand before the deletion the program exists for: with the switch gone, a sweep door finishes its own result at the box scalars, and check 7 has to read these bodies.

**What needs deciding:** what the side reading of a finished body over a box rests on. Say what a ray is and where it is posed; what the reader is entitled to know of the body it reads, and from where (the stored values alone, what the finish has certified about them, what built the body); why a quantity that is one number at every member reads wide, and whose defect that is (the reader's, the body's stored description, the scalar's, or check 7's question); whether the three items are one defect or several; and what a body whose every face moves is owed. Say what your answer does at `f64`, where today's reader is settled and another program owns the ties. If ratified text is what makes this hard, say so and what it should say instead (designer.md §2; check its provenance).
