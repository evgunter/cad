## Finding

Raised by a designer of fork `embed-price` (2026-11-18), off that
fork's question. Not weighed there.

`topo::census` describes itself as the injectivity pass tier 3 defers,
exact on lines and planes. The embedding certificate
(`topo::clearance::embedded`) is the clearance engine's injectivity
question. If PR 7068's second decision is taken (the gate confirms a
body's records and decides the meetings between its solids, that is,
tier 3′ runs inside `finish`), every finish runs both over the same
near face pairs of one solid. PR 7095's recommendation moves the
certificate's reading at a shared edge to the number the census
already reads (the cell's extent times the angle, `[topology.census]`).

`[one-way.readings]` wants one reader per fact. On planar bodies the
certificate costs 4 to 25 times tier 3
(`the-at-rest-gate-certifies-embedding`, `## Measured`), and the
census has no sweep of a curved edge against a face.

## What is not known

- The census's price at every deciding door's finish, over the suites
  (asked of the gate lane, 2026-11-18).
- Which face pairs each reader decides that the other does not, by
  kind pair.

## What is needed

Weigh it as a fork once PRs 7068 and 7095 are answered and the two
prices are measured: whether a face pair of one solid has one reader,
total over kind pairs, and which.

## One function, two callers (PR 7119, 2026-11-18)

PR 7119 makes the overlap concrete for one reading. The clearance
engine asks the census's depth gap (`census::depth_gap::gaps_in_depth`)
at a face pair's root, so the certificate and the census's arm 1 now
decide "these two faces are apart in depth" by one function, each at
its own K name (`clearance_depth_gap`, `census_backstop_depth_gap`)
and against its own bound. Where it lives and what its pad stands for
are
`the-depth-gap-lives-in-the-census-and-its-pad-is-not-derived-for-the-clearance-engine`.
