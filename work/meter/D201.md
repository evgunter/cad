## What

**The budget CSV carries no stable face identity, and the producer throws one away.** `tools/tess-meter`'s `face_rows` holds a `topo::FaceKey` in `patch.face` and writes only `enumerate()`'s ordinal, so `tess-lint`'s join has nothing but the ordinal to key on and the same-shape face pairs stay indistinguishable — `S73`'s open half. (How many, on the committed baseline: `tools/tess-lint/tests/baseline_census.rs`, the census's one home.) A `FaceKey` is an allocation ordinal in disguise, so this is not a column rename: what a DURABLE per-face name would be reaches `crates/topo` and `demos/` and must be settled before the column is added. **The design question may want an issue rather than a lane.**

## Finding

### S73. The budget gate's per-face join has no stable face identity to key on — Track K's `C15` (issue #746)

On `tools/tess-lint`, whose per-face join is by face ORDINAL because the budget CSV carries no other per-face name — the ground where the project's measure-don't-guess rule (`memories/tessellation-budget.md`) is implemented.

**The mis-join is closed.** The join runs under a pointwise precondition over the columns no rule compares — `chart`, the sizing block's presence, the trim box, the whole-patch divisions — a disagreeing ordinal is announced with the column and both readings, and ordinals below it are still compared.

**What survives is what the CSV cannot express.** Two faces of one scene agreeing on every one of those columns and swapping ordinals are indistinguishable, and they are not rare: on the committed baseline several such pairs stand, each two walls of one body with identical trim box and identical divisions.

**The count is not restated here, and that is the correction.** It is a reading of `docs/tess-budget-data/tess-budget-baseline.csv`, so it moves whenever that file is re-cut, and its three hand-transcribed copies each went stale where they could not follow. Every figure they carried was CORRECT when it was written: the baseline as committed at `632e60d76` — the commit that did the `S73` work — has exactly 1327 rows and exactly 22,545 corpus-wide pairs, which is what the copies said. The corpus-wide half went stale the same day, at the teapot re-baseline `ecdd9ec7c` (1315 rows, 22,423 pairs); the sized-row half — 8 pairs, 16 of 64 rows, 6 scenes — survived every cut until `a4eb03a` (CERT-10, 2026-09-01) made it 7, 14 and 5. Re-derive that over every committed BLOB of the path (`git rev-list --all --objects`, eleven distinct row counts from 1025 to 1330), not over `git log --follow` on one branch, which sees six of them and none of the matching one. Its **one home is `tools/tess-lint/tests/baseline_census.rs`**, which states the sweep's definition beside its result and re-derives every quantity — rows, sized rows, scenes, pairs, and the corpus-wide comparison — from the committed baseline on each `cargo test`. A re-cut that moves the census fails there and names what moved.

The restriction doing the work is to the SIZED rows, because an unsized swap costs the slack rule nothing; the same test measures what that restriction is worth. What the cut does NOT move: among the sized rows `chart` is `nurbs` on every one, the trim box is the whole unit square on every one, and the sizing block is "present" on every one because that is what SIZED means, so **six of the eight identity entries discriminate nothing there and the pair actually separating them is `nu`/`nv`** — six plus that pair being the whole list. The test derives that split column by column from the crate's own `identity_readings` rather than spot-checking members of it. Corpus-wide the other entries do discriminate, which is what makes the reroute case real.

Closing it needs a face identity in a column of the sweep's own: the producer-side row is `D201`.

## The question for Ev (METER, 2026-09-07)

**The premise this row was filed on has changed, and the change makes
it a much smaller question than "what is a durable per-face name".**
The row says such a name "must be settled before the column is added"
and reaches `crates/topo` and `demos/`. But the project already HAS a
ratified durable name and it is not in `topo`:
`editor_core::StableName` (N1) is *"a derivation path — the minting
node plus an op-typed role path. Float-free and arena-key-free by
construction"* (`crates/editor-core/src/names/role.rs:79-86`). The
lookup this row needs already exists in both directions — the
`NameTable` maps an `EntityRef` carrying `EntityKey::Face(FaceKey)` to
a `StableName` (`crates/editor-core/src/names/emit.rs`), and
`editor_core::all_faces(ev, node)` hands back a node's face names
(`crates/editor-core/src/names/mod.rs:97`). So `FaceKey → StableName`
is a solved problem in this tree.

**What is actually missing is one hop in the tour.** The sweep is
`demos/tour/src/tessbudget.rs`, which reads `sb.body` and calls
`tess_meter::face_rows(delta, &sb.body, &mesh, &measures)`. `SceneBody`
(`demos/tour/src/main.rs:81-101`) holds `body: Body<f64>` and no
evaluation, so at the moment the row is written there is no
`RecipeNodeId` and no name table in scope — the tour has thrown the
document away by then. **The design question is not what a face name
is; it is whether the SWEEP should be able to reach the one that
exists.**

### The fork

**A — carry the evaluation into the sweep.** `SceneBody` gains the
node's evaluation (or just its name table) beside the body, and the
meter writes a `StableName` column. This is the answer that actually
closes `C15`: the join keys on a name that survives a face reorder.
Costs: `demos/` is Track X, so the seam is drawn with them; scene
constructors that build a `Body` outside the document layer have no
evaluation to hand over and would need one or an honest absence;
`StableName`'s `Display` is prose (*"face name minted by node 3"*), so
the column needs a structural rendering — it is `serde`-serializable,
which is the obvious route, but a CSV column wants something flatter.

**B — a body-local name from D5 provenance.** Every entity carries a
birth record (`crates/topo/src/provenance.rs`), which looks like the
cheap answer because it needs nothing above `topo`. **It is not one,
and it is named here so it is rejected on the record rather than
re-proposed:** the site payloads ARE arena keys and are explicitly
*"body-lineage-scoped"*, so a provenance record does not survive the
reorder either. It is an allocation ordinal one level of indirection
away.

**C — ratify that the budget CSV carries no face name.** Accept `C15`'s
residue as permanent: the same-shape pairs stay indistinguishable, and
the guard is `compare`'s rule-4 precondition, which announces a re-key
rather than mis-comparing (unit 0 of this program is verifying exactly
that). The honest cost is the one `C15` already measured — among the
SIZED rows six of the eight identity entries are constant, and only
`nu`/`nv` separate a pair, so the precondition is thinnest exactly
where the gate does its work.

### The recommendation, and the one thing not measured

**A, scoped**: the name exists, the lookup exists, and the missing
piece is one field on a demo struct. It would be a strange outcome for
the project to design a second identity scheme for one CSV when N1 is
ratified and reachable.

**What has NOT been measured, and would be the lane's first act:** how
many tour scenes could actually hand over an evaluation. Ten of the
tour's thirty-four scene modules mention the document layer at all, and
a module is not a scene — the real number could be most of the corpus
or a minority of it. If it is a minority, A degrades into "a name
column that is empty for most rows", which is worth knowing before
choosing it, and C becomes more attractive than it looks here. The
count was left unmeasured deliberately rather than estimated, because
guessing it is exactly the shape of error this row exists to punish.

**Kind note stands.** This remains an `issue`, not a `unit`: whichever
arm is chosen, the work that follows is a lane in `demos/` (Track X's
seam) and a column in `tools/tess-meter`, and those are dispatched
after the answer, not before it.
