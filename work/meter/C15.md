## Finding

### S73. The budget gate's per-face join has no stable face identity to key on — Track K's `C15` (issue #746)

On `tools/tess-lint`, whose per-face join is by face ORDINAL because the budget CSV carries no other per-face name — the ground where the project's measure-don't-guess rule (`memories/tessellation-budget.md`) is implemented.

**The mis-join is closed.** The join runs under a pointwise precondition over the columns no rule compares — `chart`, the trim box, the whole-patch divisions — a disagreeing ordinal is announced with the column and both readings, and ordinals below it are still compared.

**What survives is what the CSV cannot express.** Two faces of one scene agreeing on every one of those columns and swapping ordinals are indistinguishable, and they are not rare: on the committed baseline several such pairs stand, each two walls of one body with identical trim box and identical divisions.

**The count is not restated here, and that is the correction.** It is a reading of `docs/tess-budget-data/tess-budget-baseline.csv`, so it moves whenever that file is re-cut, and its three hand-transcribed copies each went stale where they could not follow. Re-derive that over every committed BLOB of the path (`git rev-list --all --objects`, eleven distinct row counts from 1025 to 1330), not over `git log --follow` on one branch, which sees six of them and none of the matching one. Its **one home is `tools/tess-lint/tests/baseline_census.rs`**, which states the sweep's definition beside its result and re-derives every quantity — rows, sized rows, scenes, pairs, and the corpus-wide comparison — from the committed baseline on each `cargo test`. The test derives that split column by column from the crate's own `identity_readings` rather than spot-checking members of it. ## What an undetected swap costs, and where that reading lives

### The producer side is in place and correct

`D201` landed the `name` column and the mechanism behind it. `tess_meter::face_rows` takes `names: Option<&FaceNames>`, and the two absences it can meet are not the same thing: a caller with `None` has no name source at all — a scene built outside the document layer — and every row it writes is honestly unnamed, while a caller that DID hand over a table has claimed the table is this body's, so a face missing from it is refused rather than written as an empty name. Nothing about the column says anything false in either direction, and no rule reads it yet.

### The coverage is disjoint from the defect

Reading the column discharges nothing today, because the rows it covers and the rows this defect lives in do not intersect. Only a scene whose body arrives from an evaluated document can hand a name table over; on the committed baseline no scene carrying a name carries a sized row, and not one of the same-shape pairs is named. The two sets are disjoint, so the join has nothing new to key on exactly where it needed something.

That is a reading of a committed artefact and no figure for it is transcribed here. Re-derive it by grouping the parsed rows by scene and intersecting the scenes with a non-empty `name` against the scenes carrying a sized row.

### What the swap costs, and which half is a theorem

The two halves are not the same kind of claim, and conflating them is what makes this row easy to get wrong:

- **Rule 1 cannot move — a theorem about the rule's shape.** It compares per-SCENE triangle totals, and a swap within one scene permutes the summands of one sum. No corpus can make it fire, so nothing asserts it.
- **Rule 2 does not move — a READING of this baseline.** It compares `Row::recoverable()` = `grid_cells / span_opt_cells`, and within every pair the committed corpus carries those two readings agree. A re-cut can end that at any time, which is precisely why it needs an executable home rather than a sentence.
- **`worst_dev` is REPORTED, not gated.** The gate emits five kinds — `Vanished`, `Triangles`, `Uncovered`, `Slack`, `Rekeyed` — and not one of them reads it. The `Kind` enum is what settles this; a ratio named in prose beside two gated ones is not thereby gated.

So today an undetected swap moves **no finding at all, and it does move what the report prints**: `total` = `delta / worst_dev`, and `worst_cert` in its last digits. Invisible in the gate, visible in the report.

The magnitude of that report-side movement is deliberately left unpinned and is not written here. It is a reading of an UNGATED column, so an assertion on it would fire on a re-cut that made a pair's two members agree — an improvement, not a defect. Re-derive it when it is wanted: take `delta / worst_dev` for each member of each pair and read the relative gap.

### The home

`tools/tess-lint/tests/baseline_census.rs`, beside the pair census it already holds — the standing rule is that a census has one executable home and every other site points at it, and this row's own history above is why. It hands each pair's swap to `compare` and requires an empty `Report`, so a re-cut that makes a swap gate-visible fails there and names the pair and the finding; it then requires the two recoverable slacks to agree exactly, so a sub-tolerance drift reds before the gate-visible one does; and it requires that no pair is separated by `name`, so the day a scene carrying a pair becomes document-built the failure says that `C15` has become dischargeable for those pairs and what to do about it.

### Why this stays open

The defect is live: the CSV still cannot tell those faces apart, and the consumer-side join still keys on the ordinal. What closes it is corpus-side — a scene that carries a pair becoming document-built, so that the sweep has an evaluation to take names from — and Ev's ruling on `D201` (*"it's a demo so it doesn't matter much"*) scopes that as not urgent, which is not the same as done.
