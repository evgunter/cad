## What

**The budget instrument's own resolution exceeds its consumer's whole tolerance — CONFIRMED by measurement, and this is a defect in the gate rather than in the meter.** `D105` boxes the split scan's excess on the **continuous** objective at 2.09% (a class sup, now analytic). `tess-lint` reads the **`ceil`'d** column, and there the same scan is far worse: the shipped family's `anisotropic, live cross term` member scores **5.8824%**, already above `tess_lint::GROWTH_TOLERANCE − 1 = 5%`, and along a *single smooth geometry change* — `mvv` scaled 1× → 100× at realistic counts, 10,580 → 105,760 — the scan/true ratio runs 1.00000 → **1.05948**. **So a face whose bound moves relative to the scan lattice under a pure geometry change moves `span_opt_cells` past the gate's entire margin from the instrument alone**, and the budget gate can fire, or fail to fire, on lattice placement rather than on tessellation. **The lever is cheap and measured**: at 8 decades, `SPLIT_SCAN_SAMPLES ≥ 379` puts the one-sided envelope under 5% (the declined narrowing to 3.7 decades would give 2.70%). Deliberately **not** taken by `D105`, because raising it moves every committed budget number and re-cuts `docs/tess-budget-data/`, which is a unit of its own. Its first question is which lever, and its second is whether a gate whose margin is the same order as its instrument's resolution was measuring what it thought. **The second lever is blocked on something nobody has**: narrowing the range to 3.7 decades brings the continuous excess to 2.70% with every `D105` claim green, and the reason not to is that **nothing in the tree characterises what `muu/mvv` ratios real certified bounds produce** — so the range question needs that characterisation first, while the sample-count lever costs no range at all.

## Closed

Landed with `D201` as METER unit 6, branch
`meter/split-scan-and-face-name`. **The sample-count lever, taken; the
range lever, left where the row left it.**

`tess_meter::SPLIT_SCAN_SAMPLES` is **379**. The threshold was
re-derived rather than inherited: the one-sided envelope
`10^(decades/(samples − 1)) − 1` at `SPLIT_SCAN_DECADES = 8` reads
**5.0075% at 378** and **4.9939% at 379**, so 379 is the smallest count
that puts the whole envelope inside `tess_lint::GROWTH_TOLERANCE − 1`.

**What the two closed forms say at the new pair**, both re-computed by
`unfloored_worst_excess` / `floored_worst_excess`, which take
`(decades, samples)` and nothing else: unfloored **0.16573% →
0.11876%**, floored **2.09180% → 1.75540%**. An independent random
search over 400,000 floored bounds found **1.75256%** under the second,
which replaces the two searches the docs carried against the old pair
(they read 2.0768% and 2.0918%, both of which sit ABOVE the new bound
and would have been false left in place).

**On the `ceil`'d column the gate actually reads** — the reason this
row exists — the family's worst falls from **5.8824% to 2.94%**, and
the member the row named (`anisotropic, live cross term`) from
**5.8824% to 0.5249%**. Both are readings over eight bounds, not
bounds over a class, and the `ceil` lottery is unchanged (379: 2.94%,
380: 4.11%, 381: 1.95%, 382: 2.03%, 383: 3.19%; 2,000 samples still
0.79%).

**Scan-to-true ratios along a scaled member are SAMPLES of the range
they name, and this row's were quoted as though they bounded it.**
`mvv` scaled 1× → 100× on that member, shipped scan against a
40,001-point reference on the same range: at 379 samples 301 sample
points reach **1.02956** and 4,001 reach **1.03241**, against the
1.02320 first recorded; at 321, 4,001 points reach **1.06151** against
the 1.05948 in this row's `## What`. Nothing says a denser sweep stops
there. The figures are readings at a stated density and are
recorded as that in `tess_meter::SPLIT_SCAN_DECADES`' docs, where the
figure they correct used to sit.

**WHAT THIS ROW DID NOT ACHIEVE, corrected.** The first version of this
section said the raised count puts the `ceil` lottery "entirely inside
the consumer's margin instead of straddling it". **That is false, and
it is the same error this row was opened about, one level up.** The
one-sided envelope `10^(decades/(samples − 1)) − 1` bounds the
CONTINUOUS excess; it does not bound the `ceil`'d one, and nothing
does. `tools/tess-meter/tests/derivations.rs`'s
`the_ceild_excess_can_exceed_the_one_sided_envelope` exhibits a bound
in closed numbers — `muu = 100, muv = 0, mvv = 0.1` over a `1 × 10` box
at `δ_s = 1`, where an admissible 13 × 5 grid at `t = 26` costs 65
cells and the shipped scan reports **70** — whose `ceil`'d excess is
**7.6923%**, over the envelope's 4.9939% AND over the gate's whole 5%
margin. One missed division out of `n` costs `1/n`, so the smallest
excess a miss can cost is set by the coarsest axis of the answer, not
by how fine the lattice is:
on the committed baseline the median per-ANALYSIS-CELL optimum is
**44.4 cells**, near seven divisions an axis, with **56 of the 64 sized
faces averaging under 100** — where one whole division is 14%.

**So what this row achieved, stated exactly.** The CONTINUOUS half of
the instrument's excess — the half the two constants govern smoothly —
now fits inside the consumer's margin where at 321 samples it did not
(5.9254% → 4.9939%), and 379 is the smallest count that does it, which
is now re-derived on every run by
`the_shipped_sample_count_is_the_smallest_whose_envelope_fits` instead
of standing in prose. On the family it also takes the `ceil`'d worst
from 5.88% to 2.94%. What it did NOT do is bound the quantity
`tools/tess-lint` divides by. **The premise in `## What` — that the
budget gate can fire, or fail to fire, on lattice placement rather than
on tessellation — therefore SURVIVES this row**, smaller by the
continuous half and by the family's measured improvement, but not
closed. It is re-filed as
`tess-lint-growth-margin-unprotected-from-ceil-quantisation`.

**The range lever is untouched and the block is unchanged.** Narrowing
to 3.7 decades would bring the same envelope to 2.2794%, and nothing in
the tree yet characterises what `muu/mvv` ratios real certified bounds
produce, so the question the row parked it on is still parked. The
sample count costs no range at all, which is why it was the one to
take.

**Every hard-coded percentage moved** — see the PR body for the table.
One assertion needed widening rather than re-deriving:
`derivations.rs`'s *"the reference stopped being the better answer"*
compared the bracketing oracle against the scan with no float
allowance, and on the `cross term only` member — whose cost is
CONSTANT in `t` — the two evaluate one real number by different routes.
At 379 the scan lands one ulp below the oracle and the row went red on
dust. It now carries a `1e-12` relative slack, five orders under the
smallest real excess in the family, with the reason at the assertion.
