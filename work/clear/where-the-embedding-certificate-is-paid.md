CARVE's `self-overlapping-spines-build-and-validate` (P0) puts the
remedy in a certificate on the built body: the body-level injectivity
question in `topo`. With NETCACHE (PR 6288) it answers on every tour
loft and sweep, at 0.04–2.3 s (10.1 s on the Klein loop), against builds
of 2–24 ms.

Designers weighed where that price is paid and at which scalars (the
blinding is on `analysis/design-fork/loft-door-embedding`). The `[ev]`
PR states the question and is updated in place with the answer.

## The question

1. **Where the certificate is paid.** In the loft and sweep doors, or in
   the at-rest gate, as part of what a finished body (`AtRestBody`)
   promises: a boundary embedded off its recorded contacts. The diff is
   the second: `docs/DESIGN.md` `[topology.tiers]`, the finished-body
   paragraph.
2. **Whether f64 certifies.** #1737's option B gave f64 no clearance lane
   because an f64 body could not then be lifted to Interval. The engine
   now lifts every carrier exactly, and the f64 and Interval answers
   agree on every body measured. The recommendation is that the yes/no
   verdict reads certification rights, f64 included. The minimum
   measure keeps its `None` at f64, and shell's f64 cavity window closes
   with it.

Three designers converged on both. What lands with it, as the
carrying-out of ratified text rather than a new decision:
- the gate's verdict kept per node by content key (PR 3870's "once");
- preview landings that do not finish the product.

D5 is unchanged. The drag stall when a boolean consumes a loft is named
as a residual.
