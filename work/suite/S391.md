## Finding

**Raised by lane T-a.** `sweep/tests/common/orient.rs`'s `stack_axis` returns
the chord of `side_faces[0][0]` from `v = 0` to `v = 1` — one WALL's diagonal,
which equals the stacking direction only when the chart does not roll. On the
authored-roll loft, whose stack is exactly `+z`, it reads
`(-0.999, 0.95, 1.0)`: about 55 degrees off. Its two readers are the helix
suite's not-orientable-against-the-chord guard and the fixed-chord index
itself, and neither is wrong today — both want *some* fixed reference and
assert against it — but the NAME says stack and the value is a wall diagonal,
which is how a later row picks it up for a question it cannot answer. Lane T-a
hit exactly that and measured its way back out; the roll row now takes its axis
from the level rings' centroids, which is the stack whatever the walls do.

The fix is a rename plus a doc sentence, or a second helper that returns the
centroid chord; it is not this lane's row because both live readers would need
re-arguing and neither is broken.

**Confidence**: sure (measured). **Where**:

## Closed by the rename (2026-09-15, PR 2624)

`stack_axis` is `first_wall_chord`. The body is unchanged — it still
returns the chord of `side_faces[0][0]` from `v = 0` to `v = 1` at
mid-`u` — and no assertion's truth value moved anywhere in the tree.

**The row's own premise was wrong, and that is why the rename was the
whole fix.** The row supposed that "two of its readers want the stack".
Neither does. `level_plane`'s orientability guard wants *a* fixed
reference, not a correct one: its question is whether some fixed vector
orients every level the same way, so a candidate clearing the bar at
every level **is** the witness that a fixed axis exists. And
`m8_14_long_turn_sweep`'s guard wants *index 1's own* reference — it
takes the worst `|cos|` over `LevelIndex`'s planes to prove the helix
rows reach past index 1 rather than restating it, so the vector measured
against has to be the one index 1 actually computes, not a better one.
An assertion against the true stack would have been a fact about an axis
nothing uses. So the row's second arm — a second helper returning the
centroid chord — was not merely the more expensive fix, it was the wrong
one; and no third spelling was minted, because
`turning_orientation.rs` already carries both the end-to-end
(`ring_centroid` at `v = 0` and `v = 1`) and per-step (`spine_chords`)
spellings of the genuine stack.

**The number the row carried was measured rather than transcribed.** The
row said "about 55 degrees off"; for `twisted_lofted(0.05)` the chord is
`(-0.99875, 0.95, 1)`, **54.05°** off `+z`, and the three authored thetas
give `cos = 0.587 / 0.707 / 0.794` against the guard's `0.1`. The doc
now names the θ it quotes, because the fixture is a family and the
sentence was false at two of its three members.

**What the style review caught, and the unit took.** The rename was
undone one line later at both live call sites (`let axis = …`), which is
this program's standing question answered in the wrong direction; both
bindings are `chord` now. The retired noun survived in two files the
unit had declared swept — `LevelIndex`'s doc eight lines under a line
the diff edited, and `turning_orientation.rs` twice — and the PR's
"read in full" prose claim was withdrawn as false and replaced with a
literal-phrase pattern and its hit list. Four doc sentences were wrong
or over-claiming and are fixed: one attributing the 54° divergence to
the fixture's authored θ = 0.05 when the effective roll is θ − π/2
(at a genuine 0.05 roll the chord is 2.86° off), one saying
`ring_centroid` "averages every wall" when it reads `side_faces[0]`
only and multi-loop lofts exist, one saying "any fixed vector serves"
when the index needs the `v = 0` → `v = 1` sense, and one asserting an
invariant nothing enforces ("no caller may read it as one") over a
`Vec3<f64>` indistinguishable from a stack vector — the same mechanism
that failed for `stack_axis` in the first place.

**Residue, with its own file.** The argument "what the fixed-chord index
needs, and why a turning body has none" is spelled at six sites across
three files, and `orient.rs` is 824 lines carrying two containment
oracles, a declared ray-parity copy and ten derived constants. The
module header routes *items* and nothing routes *prose*. Filed as
`work/tint/orient-module-prose-accumulation.md` on S-TINT's slate,
which owns `crates/*/tests/*`.
