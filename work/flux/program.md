PROPS' successor, opened when PROPS' slate had outgrown it a second time:
the closed-form area and volume arms over curved surfaces, and the
`geom-core` scalar and spline doors they stand on. PROPS closed on the
refusal-text and recourse family — what a refusal *says* — and handed
over everything about what the kernel can *measure*.

The program's centre of gravity is the four hard rows PROPS never
reached: a face bounded by a `Curve3::Spiric` has no area or volume lane
at all (which is where the hollowed klein elbow and the sectioned torus
vessel stop today), the sphere flux arm's coplanar premise leaves the
lune family outside tier 3, the wedge arm refuses a meridian that
arrives in lineage pieces where the torus arm folds them, and
`props_rim_side`/`props_rim_dir_group` record facts about cycle order
rather than about the face. Under them sit the scalar and spline doors —
certified-door coverage, the refinement class PROPS closed one site of,
the hand-spelled mignitude and refuse-then-read readers — because a flux
arm is only as honest as the door it reads through.

Posture inherited from PROPS and worth restating, because this program's
whole subject is where it has been bitten: **a premise asserted in a
spec is the failure mode of record here.** One premise about `du_of_rims`
failed three times running — asserted in a spec, inherited by a unit
that shipped a MAJOR through it, then patched by a guard that checked a
sum where a cover was needed and shipped that to main. A spec in this
program requires a decide where PROPS' specs asserted one.
