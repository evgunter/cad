**Who may read a bracket, and what they learn.** Two P1 rows carry the
story. An interval's endpoint can be read without asking whether the
enclosure certified
(`an-interval-endpoint-can-be-read-without-asking-whether-it-certified`,
a design row filed at Ev's request: make forgetting `is_certified()`
impossible by construction; of the endpoint census's fifteen main reds
in a month, three were real forgotten asks). And the certification
files hold body data beside certification
(`the-certification-files-hold-body-data-beside-certification`), so a
bound that means "certifying scalar" is written on code that is
neither. Start with the first: it is a fork, so it goes to designers
and then to Ev, and its answer decides what
`ring-refusal-readers-are-spelled-by-hand-at-every-site` and most of
the census become.

Behind them, at P2 and below: the box driver's readings (a drive that
compares certificate counts as branches, a gate rejection that bisects
to budget, a replay that nearly certifies, the race row's leaves), the
ledger entries still argued as the weakest bound that works, and the
census rows whose bracket read re-enters the scalar.
