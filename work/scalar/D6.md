## Question

`enters_material` and `sector_shape`; a differently-shaped sweep

right.

## Put to Ev (2026-09-12, SCALAR's first `[ev]` sitting)

**What the tree says, corrected.** There is no sense-sign newtype. What
#665 typed is the PRODUCT: `geom_brep::enters::OutwardNormal<T>`, whose
only constructor `from_chart(chart_normal, sense: bool)` takes the
**bit**, and whose doc argues why a `T` sign is the wrong parameter (it
admits `1.0` on a reversed face, a dot product, an `Interval` that is
not ±1). The ±1 itself is minted at one door, `Face::sense_sign<T>()`,
and still crosses five function boundaries as a bare `T`
(`classify_material_pairing`, `material_kappa_rel`,
`rim_wedge::classify_shared_rim`, `curved_face`, `props::curved::sphere`)
and is hand-multiplied into a chart normal at ten sites across `topo`,
`sweep`, `editor-core` and `mesh` — a census `face_normal.rs` pins by
hand and whose failure message names this row. Evidence:
`work/scalar/log.md`, the D6 entry.

**Recommendation: the newtype goes all the way, and it is not a ±1
type.** #665's principle is the ruling: the sense is a bit and the typed
thing is what the bit selects. So:

1. The five doors take `sense: bool` (they compute the gradient
   themselves, so `OutwardNormal` cannot be handed in); the multiply
   becomes a conditional negation, exact in IEEE, so a D9 bit-identity
   differential is the pin.
2. The ten hand-multiplies fold onto `OutwardNormal::from_chart` /
   `face_outward_normal`; the residue where a scalar (an area, a
   winding) is genuinely signed spells the bit as a conditional
   negation.
3. `Face::sense_sign<T>()` is then retired, and with it the hand-kept
   census.
4. `sphere`'s `s_f` is two things spelled as one `T` — the face sense on
   one branch, a DECIDED rim side (`t_sign(Sign)`, which can be zero) on
   the other. They split into a bit and a `Sign`; a lane checks that
   nothing relied on the zero.

**Rejected:** a `SenseSign<T>` newtype with inhabitants ±1. It buys what
the bit already buys and re-admits the `T`-valued sign #665 argued
against (an `Interval` ±1 is an interval; a `Dual` ±1 carries a
meaningless tangent). Also rejected: leaving the five doors as they are
— the survey found one of their callers defaulting a MISSING face to
`T::one()` (`verify_tangent_declaration`; filed on BOOL's slate), which
is exactly the call shape a bit-typed door makes a reader notice.

**Cost:** ~23 code sites in 16 functions, five signatures, five crates;
two or three units after the ruling, doors first. **Question:** (a) is
that the principle — bit and product, never a ±1 type, `sense_sign`
retired; (b) does `sphere`'s mixed sign split.
