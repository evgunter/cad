Filed by SEAL from fork `seat-vertex-one-interface` (2026-11-09). All three designers agreed: once the kernel reads a declared face–face pair as one two-sided wall, every boolean runs on the whole body and carries the seat through its lineage. At that point `crates/editor-core/src/eval/wire/distribute.rs` is a second implementation of D10's "`−` and `∩` distribute over `⊔`". It is also the one that re-decides the seat (`a-cut-distributed-over-a-seat-re-decides-the-seat-and-reports-its-split-tool`).

Retiring it needs the kernel's result to say which operand solid each result solid came from, so that the editor can still key pieces by read, for picks and names (ASSEMBLY A2: piece structure is recipe structure, derived here from lineage). See `work/seal/a-vertex-piercing-two-declared-coincident-faces-reads-as-one.md` § Design.

- 2026-11-09 — opened: its trigger closed. The kernel reads a declared seat as one wall for every op the distribution covers (`⊔ − t`, `⊔ ∩ t`, `t ∩ ⊔`), at tier 3 and 3′ with the record carried (`crates/topo/tests/a_vertex_through_a_declared_seat.rs`), where the seat is an area record of two planar faces. A curved seat (a shaft in a bore) is not read as a wall (`work/seal/a-declared-wall-is-read-only-by-a-vertex-crossing-it.md`), and a seat the operation divides along a ring refuses `CarryRefused`: the distribution stays the editor's only path for those until that row lands. A `⊔` as a cut's tool still refuses (`ToolSeatCrossed`, `work/seal/a-cut-across-its-tools-seat-leaves-a-sliver-between-the-seats-two-sections.md`), which the distribution never built either.
