**Verdict:** ACCEPTED (Ev, 2026-08-18) — "lots of other great catches."
Steelman pass commissioned on one specific question: `tools/k-lint` is
the repo's own lint crate, so the capability exists — why are these rules
greps rather than lints or types? Some CI greps are the right tool
(`memories/interval-square-poison.md` documents one that caught a real

**Steelman (2026-08-18): SURVIVES IN PART — and the premise "greps instead of
types" is largely false.**

*What the types actually do.* The type-level encoding is real and does most of
the work: `Real` is comparison-free **by construction**; `Bounds`/`Decide`/
`SpanLocate` are separate subtraits; **`Bounds` has no `Dual` impl**, so the
whole bracket-reading surface is uninstantiable at duals; and three static lane
splits with refusing arms exist (S3). *(That middle clause was true when this
steelman was written on 2026-08-18 and is false as of the **D1** ruling of
2026-08-19: `Bounds` is implemented for `Dual`, and what is uninstantiable at a
dual is the `CertifiedEnclosure`-bounded surface. The steelman's conclusion is
unaffected — the type-level encoding still does most of the work — so the
sentence is amended rather than rewritten.)* Each ledger amendment litigates a *type*
question, and M5 PR 12 **explicitly costs the type-level fix and rejects it on
the merits** (*"would have had an EMPTY refusing side"*). The greps enforce only
the residue — **which files may *name* a bound** — which Rust cannot express
across crate boundaries, since sealing controls who may *implement*, not who may
*name*. Nobody has written that down.

*Two gates could not be types or lints at all.* The interval-square rule guards
a **whole-program** property (now
`scripts/gates/interval-square-allowlist.sh`, `ci.yml:437` when this was
written: *"whether THIS enclosure can
straddle zero is a global property of upstream callers that refactors change
silently"*), and the env ban has a measured receipt. And `EvalScalar` is
evidence the encoded alternative was **tried and needed more grep, not less**:
now `scripts/gates/evalscalar-allowlist.sh`, `ci.yml:354` then — *"the trait is
`pub`… so without this step any file in any crate
could acquire a compound Bounds bound invisibly to the grep above."*

*The brief's premise about `tools/k-lint` was wrong, and it matters.* `k-lint`
is a **CSV gate** over probe sweeps, not a source lint; it does not parse Rust.
**The repo has zero Rust-AST lint capability today.**

*What does not survive.* (1) **A lint/tool alternative was never evaluated** —
the only mention is a one-line "optional escalation" in `M0-LOG.md:74`, dropped
silently; no `dylint`, `clippy::disallowed*`, `clippy.toml` or proc-macro
appears anywhere in the history. The record is silence, not rejection.
(2) The greps' own remaining defects go unfixed, and #626 — which moved every
gate into `scripts/gates/` and gave each a `--selftest` both CI halves invoke

### The evaluation: `scripts/gates/README.md`

The steelman asked which of these rules are greps because a grep is the
right tool and which because nobody looked. The answer is written down
as the design page for this directory, present tense.

**The evaluation and the judgement are separated on that page**, because
they are not the same kind of claim. What each alternative CATCHES and
what it COSTS are facts about the tools and this tree, and they are what
`S13` commissioned; weighing the one gap a lint would close against the
three costs of closing it is a judgement, and the page records it as an
explicit RECOMMENDATION — *the four gates stay greps and the alias gap
stays registered where it is disclosed* — with ratification named as
Ev's. A standing rule about how a gate's blind spot may be closed was
drafted alongside it and REMOVED: a rule Ev has not ratified does not
belong on a design page.

**This row closes with the evaluation, not with the ruling.** Its two
asks are met — the three named defects probed and dispositioned, the
lint alternative actually evaluated and written down. The ratification
rides **PR 2067**, an `[ev]` PR that lists the page in `docs/DESIGN.md`'s
companion table and asks Ev to ratify or overrule the recommendation.
That PR also raises the convention question the row surfaces:
`scripts/gates/README.md` is the first design page outside
`crates/<crate>/README.md`, because the code these invariants govern is
a script directory and not a crate.

- `clippy::disallowed_*` is free and the wrong shape: it bans a PATH,
  and none of the four rules is "this path may not appear" — each is
  conditional on a composition or a file.
- A proc-macro sees only the item it is attached to, so the rule would
  hold exactly where someone remembered to opt in. Rejected on the
  mechanism.
- A `syn`-based `tools/` binary buys a parser instead of a lexer, which
  is the part `lib.sh`'s statement view already buys (S158's ruling),
  and no name resolution — so the alias case stays open — for the price
  of a build in the discipline job.
- `dylint` is the only candidate that closes anything: a `LateLintPass`
  reads bounds after resolution, so the compound-bound-through-alias
  case (`trait ArcCarrierScalar: Decide + Bounds`, then
  `T: ArcCarrierScalar`) and `include!`d text and `macro_rules!` bodies
  all come into view. Its costs are a nightly toolchain pinned against
  `rustc_private` beside the repo's stable pin, a compile step in a
  `discipline` job that runs no cargo today, and the CI-half parity
  rule — a lint runs where cargo runs, so one rule leaves the roster
  `gate-roster.sh` derives from this directory and the property that
  gate exists to hold stops being a property of the whole set.

**Which greps are the right tool**: `interval-square-allowlist.sh`
(the steelman's concession, `memories/interval-square-poison.md` — a
whole-program property no type and no lint decides), `no-ambient-env.sh`
(a text ban with a measured receipt), and the naming rules
`bounds-allowlist.sh` / `evalscalar-allowlist.sh` /
`no-extra-real-bounds.sh` enforce, because *which files may name a
bound* is a question about file paths. **Where a grep is not the right
tool is one case, not four**: the compound bound reached through a
name, shared by two of the gates and disclosed in both, and it does not
buy the three costs above.
