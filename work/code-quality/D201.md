## What

**The budget CSV carries no stable face identity, and the producer throws one away.** `tools/tess-meter`'s `face_rows` holds a `topo::FaceKey` in `patch.face` and writes only `enumerate()`'s ordinal, so `tess-lint`'s join has nothing but the ordinal to key on and the same-shape face pairs stay indistinguishable — `S73`'s open half. (How many, on the committed baseline: `tools/tess-lint/tests/baseline_census.rs`, the census's one home.) A `FaceKey` is an allocation ordinal in disguise, so this is not a column rename: what a DURABLE per-face name would be reaches `crates/topo` and `demos/` and must be settled before the column is added. **The design question may want an issue rather than a lane.**

Kind note: filed as `issue` rather than `unit` because the row itself says the durable-name design question must be settled before the column can be added, and may want an issue rather than a lane.

## Finding

### S73. The budget gate's per-face join has no stable face identity to key on — Track K's `C15` (issue #746)

On `tools/tess-lint`, whose per-face join is by face ORDINAL because the budget CSV carries no other per-face name — the ground where the project's measure-don't-guess rule (`memories/tessellation-budget.md`) is implemented.

**The mis-join is closed.** The join runs under a pointwise precondition over the columns no rule compares — `chart`, the sizing block's presence, the trim box, the whole-patch divisions — a disagreeing ordinal is announced with the column and both readings, and ordinals below it are still compared.

**What survives is what the CSV cannot express.** Two faces of one scene agreeing on every one of those columns and swapping ordinals are indistinguishable, and they are not rare: on the committed baseline several such pairs stand, each two walls of one body with identical trim box and identical divisions.

**The count is not restated here, and that is the correction.** It is a reading of `docs/tess-budget-data/tess-budget-baseline.csv`, so it moves whenever that file is re-cut — it moved at `a4eb03a` (CERT-10, 2026-09-01) with three hand-transcribed copies of it going on saying the old number, and the corpus-wide half of those copies matched no committed cut of the file even on the day it was written. Its **one home is `tools/tess-lint/tests/baseline_census.rs`**, which states the sweep's definition beside its result and re-derives every quantity — rows, sized rows, scenes, pairs, and the corpus-wide comparison — from the committed baseline on each `cargo test`. A re-cut that moves the census fails there and names what moved.

The restriction doing the work is to the SIZED rows, because an unsized swap costs the slack rule nothing; the same test measures what that restriction is worth. What the cut does NOT move: among the sized rows `chart` is `nurbs` on every one and the trim box is the whole unit square on every one, so **five of the eight identity entries discriminate nothing there and the pair actually separating them is `nu`/`nv`**. Corpus-wide the other entries do discriminate, which is what makes the reroute case real.

Closing it needs a face identity in a column of the sweep's own: the producer-side row is `D201`.
