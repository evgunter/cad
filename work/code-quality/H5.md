### S1. Two interval arithmetics, and the ratified one is absent from shipped builds

- **Where**: `crates/geom-core/src/ring_interval.rs:107`, `crates/geom-core/src/interval.rs:126`, `interval-transcendentals/src/interval.rs:44`, `docs/GENERICS-BUILD-COST.md:95`
- **Confidence**: sure

`RingInterval` reimplements outward-rounded interval arithmetic — `±`, `×`, `÷`, `powi`, `sqr`, `hull`, `from_bounds`, `lo`/`hi`, `contains`, including its own `down1`/`up1` restatement of `interval-transcendentals`' `round.rs` technique — that `DInterval` already provides. `RingInterval` is always compiled and carries ~600 references across `geom-brep`, `mesh`, `topo`, `geom` and `geom-core::spline`. `Interval` — Q1's *ratified* certification scalar — has 37 `cfg`-gated `src` sites and, per the repo's own measurement, **zero symbols in a default build**. The two disagree on semantics a caller must know: `RingInterval` has no decoration channel, no empty set, poisons on a zero-touching divisor where `Interval` clamps-and-decorates, and requires `sqr()` instead of `x * x`. The module doc's "two interval roles, deliberately distinct" argues the split; what the code shows is that the certification arithmetic actually running in shipped builds is the second type, and the first never displaced it.

**Verdict:** ACCEPTED (Ev, 2026-08-18). "RingInterval probably isn't earning its keep. Probably it should be removed and we should only use the full `Interval` — but I'd want to double check that there's no hidden cost." Steelman pass commissioned.

**Steelman (2026-08-18): SURVIVES IN PART — the core claim is confirmed and strengthened; two sub-claims are wrong; and the obstacle to acting is not the one anyone expected.** The premise (LGPL/inari on the default path) died 2 h 44 m before the type merged: PR #127 (inari → in-house backend) merged 2026-07-28T03:24:19Z, PR #130 (`RingInterval`) 2026-07-28T06:08:41Z; no document since #127 re-derives the split on any other ground. `crates/geom-brep/src/ssi/enclose.rs:7` defends ring-only certification with *"always compiled, no feature gate, no LGPL"*, committed two days after the LGPL left the tree. The tree hand-rolled transcendentals over the ring (`props/quad.rs:157` `cos_step`/`sin_step`, `:829` `sqrt_enclosure`, `:383` `AbsEnclosure`). The keep-argument is circular (`GENERICS-BUILD-COST.md:421` ↔ `ring_interval.rs:11`). **Two sub-claims do not survive**: (1) `sqr()`-vs-`x * x` is the general interval-lane rule, not a difference between the types; (2) "~600 references" overstates — measured **535 `src` references in 15 files**, five files carrying 60% (`props/quad.rs` 177, `mesh/nurbs_cert.rs` 66, `ssi/enclose.rs` 57, `spline/compose{,/tensor}.rs` 102; `topo` 13, one file). **The real obstacle: the decoration channel does not survive the `Enclosure` seam** — the seam is `lo()`/`hi()` only (`real.rs:564`), `RingInterval` puts failure in the endpoints (NaN), `Interval` puts it in the decoration and `impl Bounds for Interval` (`interval.rs:451`) forwards `self.0.lo()` without consulting it; `[-2,-1]/[0,1] = [-inf,-1]`, `hi() = -1` (`interval-transcendentals/src/arith.rs:89`) where the ring poisons — so a blind swap **converts a refusal into a pass on exactly the case the ring was specified to refuse**. Build cost is ~zero (24 s vs 24 s). Real costs: re-pinning every certificate number (#130's `2.8e-14 m²`, the 12,000/12,000 planted-corruption refusals, `docs/tess-budget-data/`), ~6,600 lines of ring-specific tests across 12 files, a `DESIGN.md` Q1 revision, and an unmeasured runtime question (`DInterval` 24 bytes vs 16, no ring-vs-`DInterval` benchmark anywhere). Delete the ring and `Enclosure` collapses into `Bounds` (see S55 below). *Could not determine*: whether the ~72k cases where the ring is tighter (out of ~3M differential comparisons) are rules `DInterval` should adopt; whether any of the 15 sites depends on poison-on-zero-divisor (settled by a differential on certify/refuse verdicts, not endpoints); whether Ev ever independently wanted a decoration-free substrate.

**Handed to this finding, 2026-09-02, from a closed neighbour**: `copysign`'s placement inside a consumer (`crates/geom-core/src/interval.rs:356`, re-derive against the tree) is Tier 1 and belongs here; that `copysign` is on the `Real` surface but not in the backend `src` is recorded as a fact, and the placement question is still open.

### S3. The "lane trait" — one cargo-culted pattern, four instances, three spellings of the same answer

- **Where**: `crates/topo/src/props.rs:360`, `crates/geom-brep/src/pcurve_cache.rs:907`, `crates/geom-brep/src/edge_nurbs.rs:214`, `crates/topo/src/chart_region.rs:252`
- **Confidence**: sure. Found independently by four scans.

**Verdict:** ACCEPTED (Ev, 2026-08-18). "This is a great candidate to be collapsed." Steelman commissioned.

**Steelman cross-reference (from S2)**: at `eval/mod.rs:865` the `PropsQuadLane` bound is inert; the repo's own precedent (`docs/archive/M5-LOG.md:2869`, the fillet battery) says the lanes collapse if `Dual` leaves `Real`; the refusing impls are **Ev's own ruling** (`M5-LOG.md:3451`), so S3 is downstream of S2.

### S55. `Enclosure` is a live trait with no consumer left

- **Where**: `crates/geom-core/src/real.rs` (the trait, and its blanket `impl<T: Bounds> Enclosure for T`), `crates/geom-core/src/lib.rs` (the export), `crates/geom-core/src/ring_interval.rs` (the direct impl)
- **Importance**: low · **Confidence**: sure · **Raised by**: the W1c fix lane (#643), 2026-08-19.

`Enclosure` existed for exactly one reason: the C9 ring cannot implement `Real` and therefore `Bounds`, and `Enclosure` was the smaller trait `f64`, `Interval` and `RingInterval` could all meet at. Its only generic consumer was `geom_core::spline::hull`. **#643 moved all of `hull` to `CertifiedEnclosure`**, so the trait, its blanket impl over `T: Bounds`, its direct `RingInterval` impl and its public export all remain, and **nothing in `crates/*/src` is generic over it any more**. Either (1) it still earns its keep as the meeting point, or (2) it is now `Bounds` wearing a second name and could be spelled at the one site that needs it.

**Verdict: DEFERRED (Ev, 2026-08-19), pending the `Bounds` narrow-vs-broad split.** `Enclosure` and `CertifiedEnclosure` already *are* a narrow/broad pair in miniature. Whoever takes the split should absorb this row rather than treat it as separate work — and should weigh #643's evidence in the other direction: collapsing the two vocabularies into a supertrait produced an `E0034` ambiguity storm across `ssi/certify.rs` and was backed out. (2) is not obviously right on its own terms: the ring is a genuine second implementor.
