## Finding

### S135. Every intra-doc link under `tests/` is inert — nothing renders them, nothing checks them, and nine are already broken

(Long finding; carried: the statement, the census definition and anchor, the nine broken, the disposition and the verdict. Omitted by sub-heading/lead-in: the *"Drift, in the record of the finding about drift"* paragraph (the earlier *"285 in 106 of 465"* spelling did not re-derive; the final merge added 9 candidates across 4 files — `editor-core/tests/boolean_op_wire.rs`, `editor-core/tests/m4_pr8_k_probe.rs`, `geom-brep/tests/m5_pr7_ssi.rs`, `sweep/tests/review_d2_adv_probes.rs` — whose five new targets all resolve), and *"#831 also added four such links before removing them again"* (the in-tree precedents for plain-backtick spelling are `geom-core/src/interval.rs:62` and `interval-transcendentals/src/lib.rs:114`; the links already in those files are left alone — converting them is `D113`'s policy call).)

**[verified, #831]** S71's dangling link was invisible to `scripts/doc-gate.sh` *"because it is in a `tests/` file"*. `cargo doc` documents lib and bin targets, and the gate's own header says the consequence in its own words — *"rustdoc builds no test targets"* (`scripts/doc-gate.sh:71`), written there to justify `--all-features`. So an intra-doc link in a `tests/` file is decoration with a promise attached, on every tier, at every eps, forever.

**The census — stated once, here, and reproducible.** Every other document that wants this number points at this block rather than restating it (S176(b)'s cure). **At `cfdc1c6f`** (that branch's final merge of `origin/main`, at `c0d90bc3`): **465 test `.rs` files in the workspace, 106 of them carrying 294 bracket-link candidates on doc lines.** None of the 294 is ever resolved. The branch head carried 293 (its last edit de-linked `review_s2.rs`'s `an_enclosing_leg_forces_an_equally_enclosing_partner`), so the census moves with the PR that states it: the reproducible **definition** and a named **anchor** are the deliverable, the integer is not.

**What the sweep counts, exactly**: files tracked by git whose path ends in `.rs` and contains a `tests/` component; lines whose trimmed form starts with `///` or `//!`; every `[target]` on such a line that is not followed by `(`, whose target is identifier-shaped (`Ident(::Ident)*`, optionally wrapped in backticks). **57 of them are `#[attribute]` names written in prose** (`#[ignore]`, `#[test]`, `#[non_exhaustive]`, `#[path]`, `#[cfg]`) — link candidates by the same test, and they **resolve**, to the built-in attributes in the prelude; 21 of the same shape sit in `src/` doc lines with `doc-gate.sh` green over them. None of the nine broken ones is one.

**Nine are broken today, and nine is a FLOOR rather than a count** — the check resolves a link's ROOT and never its member path, so every `Type::method` link whose `Type` is in scope was counted as resolving without `method` being looked for at all. Three point at names that exist nowhere and six name items that exist but are out of scope where they are cited. **Cited by target rather than by line** (S176). **#831 fixed three of the nine** — `review_s2.rs`'s module-header link to `enclosing_tangency_is_constructed_not_stumbled_upon` (S71's own, a name that exists nowhere), its `Leg::tangent_point` link (a real `pub(crate)` item an integration test can never resolve), and `geom-core/tests/review_m0_pr4.rs`'s link to `powi_diverges_from_the_tight_enclosure` (renamed AND inverted by the M5 backend swap). **The other six are dispositioned in its PR and untouched**, each re-derivable by grep: `editor-core/tests/corpus/mod.rs`'s `Vocab` (never an item — the fix turns on what the sentence means), `editor-core/tests/m9_d1_r1_probes.rs`'s `m4_pr3_names`, `geom/tests/surfaces/span_window_pairing.rs`'s `SurfaceWindow` and `Span`, `step-import/tests/freecad.rs`'s `PlacedInstance` and `topo/tests/common/mod.rs`'s `BooleanDeclarations`.

**The disposition is a policy question, not a patch — and it takes a row, `D113`.** Either the form stops being used in `tests/` (it promises a link that cannot exist), or something is built that resolves it, which rustdoc cannot be asked to do for a test target. Not taking a row was wrong: this schedule carries decision-shaped rows with no patch — the `ACCEPTED, unstaffed` shape — so the channel exists and declining it was an amnesty rather than a scoping call. The decision-holder is **Ev**, and the shape is already ruled once — S61's *"a gate must be sited where it can fire on its own inputs"*; if the answer is build something, the work belongs with Track F's instruments. **The sweep's own blind spots are in #831's body**; the one that bounds the count is above, and the others (doc lines only, identifier-shaped targets only, no `benches/` or `examples/`) each only push the floor further down.

## Measured: what option (b) would cost (S-TINT orchestrator, 2026-09-15)

Ev asked what it would cost to run `cargo doc` where the tests are
built, rather than banning the form. Measured by a lane on a 4 vCPU box
with its own target dir; the tracked tree was not touched. **The cost is
not the reason to decline — the coverage is.**

**`cargo doc` cannot do it at all.** Its target selection is
`--lib --bins --bin --examples --example`; there is no `--test`.
`cargo rustdoc` does have `--test`/`--tests`, and with `autotests = false`
the workspace has **16** test targets (15 named `all`, plus
`test-utils`' `reader_census`), so the unit of work is 16 invocations.

**But cargo does not pass `--extern` for a package's own lib when
documenting its test target**, so every integration test dies at its
`use <crate>::…` with `error[E0432]`. The workaround is hand-built: read
the matching `.rmeta` out of `cargo check`'s JSON and inject it. Each lib
yields two candidate rmeta files with nothing to say which matches the
doc unit; the right one depends on feature unification (a
workspace-wide `--all-features --tests` check served 12 crates and
produced `E0277`/`E0053` storms for `editor-core`, `geom` and
`geom-brep`, which need per-package checks); and picking wrong fails as
a hundred unrelated trait errors rather than as "wrong rmeta".

**And rustdoc does not document `#[test]` items, so the gate would be
blind to a quarter of the population it is for.** Verified twice, once
by the lane and once independently at this seat, with a four-link control
crate: the links on `//!` module docs, on a plain `fn` and on a `const`
were all reported; **the link on a `#[test] fn` was silently dropped**.
**282 of 1086** candidates under `tests/` sit on `#[test]` items —
including one of the six breakages this row names,
`step-import/tests/freecad.rs`'s `PlacedInstance`, which rustdoc does
NOT catch (the same file's link on a `const` it does). Five of six
caught, and the miss is structural rather than incidental.

A gate that reports green over a class it cannot see is the shape
`scripts/doc-gate.sh`'s own header argues against at length in its
pass-3 "detector of absence" paragraph.

**Wall cost, for the record**, since it was the question asked: the
gate's pass-1 equivalent is 23 s cold on that box; a test arm adds ~54 s
(the dev-dependency closure, which `cargo doc` never builds today and
which the `fmt` job therefore does not have) plus ~114 s of rustdoc over
the 16 targets — call it **+170 s, ~8×** the current gate. Warm re-run of
the sweep alone is 16 s; one target after touching one test file is 2 s.
Scaled to the hosted 2 vCPU box (ci.yml records the doc gate at 67 s cold
/ 33 s warm) that is roughly **3–6 minutes added to `fmt`**, moving it
from a job that hangs off the change filter in parallel to one
comparable with the run's longest (`k-lint (gate)` 450 s, `fmt` 367 s,
`build` 324 s). `rust-cache` would carry the registry half after the
first run but cleans workspace-member artifacts before saving, so all
1112 workspace test files re-type-check every run.

Other costs worth recording: every workspace test target is named `all`,
so all 16 render into `target/doc/all/` and clobber each other — the HTML
is worthless and this is a lint-only pass. `--document-private-items` is
mandatory, not optional (a test target is a bin crate whose modules are
private; without it the sweep produced 2 warnings, with it 116). 19 of
111 unresolved-link diagnostics arrive with **no `file:line`** at all,
because rustdoc loses the span in `#[path]`-mounted modules. And the
cost multiplies at the pass level: `doc_pass_with` already runs two
cargo invocations per root, a test arm is a third, pass 2's outside
roots have test files too (`demos/tour` 9, `tools/k-lint` 6,
`tools/tess-meter` 6, `interval-transcendentals` 5, `tools/tess-lint` 4),
and pass 3 would need its own test arm for its own absence argument to
hold.

**What the sweep found that is worth keeping either way.** 119
diagnostics over ~107 sites in 59 files across 12 of 16 crates: 111
unresolved links, 3 `invalid_html_tags` (e.g. an unbackticked
`Dual<Interval>` in `geom-core/tests/review_m5_pr1_launder.rs`), 2
`redundant_explicit_links`. Of the 111, **62 over 62 sites are
identifier-shaped and real** (sibling test-fn names, `super::orient`,
`hull::domain_hull`, `geom_brep::SSI_CERT_SPANS`, `Tolerance`, `Eps`,
`DihedralClass::Smooth`), and **30 over 21 sites are `[0,1]`/`[u0,u1]`/
`[x0,x1]` interval and array prose** that is not a link at all and that
this row's census does not count. Per crate: sweep 35, editor-core 27,
topo 10, mesh 9, profile 7, geom-brep 7, geom-core 6, step-export 5,
geom 5, step-import 4, viewer 3, bvh 1; `pncad`, `stl`, `verbs` and
`test-utils` clean. **This is a hit list a de-linking sweep can start
from**, whichever way the decision goes.

Confirmed as this row states it: `#[attribute]` names in prose
(`ignore`, `test`, `cfg`, `path`, `non_exhaustive`) **resolve** — zero
diagnostics anywhere in the sweep.

## Closed — ruled: no format change and no mechanism (Ev, in-chat, 2026-09-15)

**The decision this row exists to take has been taken, and the answer is
that an intra-doc link in a `tests/` file is prose, checked by nothing,
and that is acceptable.** No ban, no de-linking sweep, no gate.

**Ev's argument, which is the one this row never made against itself:**
*an alternate citation format would not actually prevent drift.*
Converting `[Foo]` to `` `Foo` `` removes a promise of a hyperlink that
never rendered, and adds no check whatever. A stale ``[`Foo`]`` and a
stale `` `Foo` `` are the same defect — a name in prose that no longer
resolves — and the spelling is not what makes either one rot. This row
had framed its fork as *"the form stops being used, or something is
built that resolves it"*, which reads as two live options; the first was
never an option at all, because it changes only how the unchecked thing
is spelled.

**So the real fork was always "build a checker or don't", and the
measurement above closes the first half**: rustdoc cannot be that
checker (cargo will not even assemble the doc unit without hand-injected
externs, and rustdoc does not document `#[test]` items, which is 282 of
1086 candidates and one of this row's own six named breakages). A
repo-local checker could be written, and — this is the point — it would
key on an identifier in a doc line whichever brackets are around it, so
it never needed this decision. **The format question was a red herring
and is retired as one.**

**What this row's inertness costs, stated plainly so nobody re-opens it
by rediscovering half of it:** ~62 identifier-shaped citations under
`tests/` do not resolve today, and nothing will notice when more join
them. That is charter shape 4 and it is real. It is also **not scheduled
and not filed** — deliberately. A one-time cleanup of 62 names with no
guard behind it re-rots immediately, which is Ev's own argument applied
to the cleanup rather than to the format; paying for it would buy a
tidier tree for one commit and no durability. The hit list is in the
measurement section above and dies with this directory when the program
closes. **If that trade is ever re-taken, it is re-taken as "write the
checker", never as "fix the 62".**

**What this does NOT close.** `H12`, `S216` and `C18` share this row's
root cause — rustdoc collects nothing from a `tests/` target — and none
of them is a citation-format question. `H12`'s eleven
`compile_fail,EXXXX` blocks in `geom-core/tests/review_m0_pr2.rs` and
`review_m0_pr3.rs` are **negative proofs no tier has ever evaluated**:
each claims the compiler rejects a specific program, and the day a bound
is loosened or a method added the program starts compiling and the block
that would have gone red is not there to. That is shape 1 — a row that
cannot fail — and it is sharper than anything this row was about. The
four-sided pairing the plan records is dissolved: three sides remain and
they are about dead proofs, not dead links.
