## Finding

### S73. The budget gate's per-face join has no stable face identity to key on — Track K's `C15` (issue #746)

On `tools/tess-lint`, whose per-face join is by face ORDINAL because the budget CSV carries no other per-face name — the ground where the project's measure-don't-guess rule (`memories/tessellation-budget.md`) is implemented.

**The mis-join is closed.** The join runs under a pointwise precondition over the columns no rule compares — `chart`, the trim box, the whole-patch divisions — a disagreeing ordinal is announced with the column and both readings, and ordinals below it are still compared.

**What survives is what the CSV cannot express.** Two faces of one scene agreeing on every one of those columns and swapping ordinals are indistinguishable, and they are not rare: on the committed baseline several such pairs stand, each two walls of one body with identical trim box and identical divisions.

**The count is not restated here, and that is the correction.** It is a reading of `docs/tess-budget-data/tess-budget-baseline.csv`, so it moves whenever that file is re-cut, and its three hand-transcribed copies each went stale where they could not follow. Every figure they carried was CORRECT when it was written: the baseline as committed at `632e60d76` — the commit that did the `S73` work — has exactly 1327 rows and exactly 22,545 corpus-wide pairs, which is what the copies said. The corpus-wide half went stale the same day, at the teapot re-baseline `ecdd9ec7c` (1315 rows, 22,423 pairs); the sized-row half — 8 pairs, 16 of 64 rows, 6 scenes — survived every cut until `a4eb03a` (CERT-10, 2026-09-01) made it 7, 14 and 5. Re-derive that over every committed BLOB of the path (`git rev-list --all --objects`, eleven distinct row counts from 1025 to 1330), not over `git log --follow` on one branch, which sees six of them and none of the matching one. Its **one home is `tools/tess-lint/tests/baseline_census.rs`**, which states the sweep's definition beside its result and re-derives every quantity — rows, sized rows, scenes, pairs, and the corpus-wide comparison — from the committed baseline on each `cargo test`. A re-cut that moves the census fails there and names what moved.

The restriction doing the work is to the SIZED rows, because an unsized swap costs the slack rule nothing; the same test measures what that restriction is worth. What the cut does NOT move: among the sized rows `chart` is `nurbs` on every one and the trim box is the whole unit square on every one, so **five of the seven identity entries discriminate nothing there and the pair actually separating them is `nu`/`nv`** — five plus that pair being the whole list. The test derives that split column by column from the crate's own `identity_readings` rather than spot-checking members of it. Corpus-wide the other entries do discriminate, which is what makes the reroute case real.

Closing it needs a face identity in a column of the sweep's own: the producer-side row is `D201`.

## What an undetected swap costs, and where that reading lives

### The producer side is in place and correct

`D201` landed the `name` column and the mechanism behind it. `tess_meter::face_rows` takes `names: Option<&FaceNames>`, and the two absences it can meet are not the same thing: a caller with `None` has no name source at all — a scene built outside the document layer — and every row it writes is honestly unnamed, while a caller that DID hand over a table has claimed the table is this body's, so a face missing from it is refused rather than written as an empty name. Nothing about the column says anything false in either direction, and no rule reads it yet.

### The coverage is disjoint from the defect

Reading the column discharges nothing today, because the rows it covers and the rows this defect lives in do not intersect. Only a scene whose body arrives from an evaluated document can hand a name table over, and no scene carrying a sized row is one of them. The join has nothing new to key on exactly where it needed something.

**That predicate is a reading of a committed artefact, so it is not left standing here as prose**: `no_scene_carrying_a_sized_row_carries_a_name` intersects the two scene sets on every `cargo test`, and reds the moment any sized scene becomes document-built. No figure for it is transcribed here, for the reason this row exists.

### What the swap costs, and which half is a theorem

The two halves are not the same kind of claim, and conflating them is what makes this row easy to get wrong:

- **Rule 1 cannot move — a theorem, on a premise worth naming.** It compares per-SCENE triangle totals, and a swap within one scene permutes the summands of one sum. Permutation-invariance of a sum is FALSE for `f64`; the theorem holds because `SceneTotals::triangles` is `usize`. Its neighbour `extrapolated_triangles` is `f64` and does move in its last bit under exactly this permutation, which is what makes the premise load-bearing rather than decorative.
- **Rule 2 does not move — a READING of this baseline.** It compares `Row::recoverable()` = `grid_cells / span_opt_cells`, and within every pair the committed corpus carries those quotients agree. A re-cut can end that at any time, which is precisely why it needs an executable home rather than a sentence.
- **No `Kind` reads `worst_dev`.** The gate emits five — `Vanished`, `Triangles`, `Uncovered`, `Slack`, `Rekeyed` — and not one of them reads it. The enum is what settles this; a ratio named in prose beside two gated ones is not thereby gated.

**And the report does not move either — measured, not deduced.** A swap exchanges two whole rows of one scene, so every aggregate the report folds sees the same multiset; that is EXACT for the integer sums and no more than suggestive for the floating ones, by the same `f64` argument the rule-1 bullet above rests on. What is known is that the PRINTED report comes back byte-identical for every pair, which is a measurement over this corpus and survives in part because the last-bit move dies at the `{:.1}x` the column prints at. Two names have to be kept apart to read any of it: `Row::total_slack` IS `delta / worst_dev` but is never printed — it exists only as an addend inside `SceneTotals::add` — while `SceneTotals::total_slack`, which is what the report's `total` column prints, is `measured_triangles / extrapolated_triangles`, triangle-weighted, and its own doc rejects the identification. `worst_cert` reaches no reader at all; the arm-3 roster in `tess-lint-ungated-columns-fold-silently` is the statement of record on which ungated columns reach a reader, and this row agrees with it.

So what an undetected swap costs is **a wrong-face attribution in columns nothing compares** — not a moved number anywhere, in the gate or in the report. There is no reported-side figure to threshold, which is why nothing here is pinned: the earlier reading that put the cost at a percentage of the reported `total` ratio was wrong, and the percentage it named is the gap between the two ROWS' `delta / worst_dev`, which a swap does not change — it changes only which ordinal carries which. Re-derive the whole claim by diffing `tess-lint <csv> --top 100` across a swap of the two `face` fields, with a byte-identical copy as the control.

### The home

`tools/tess-lint/tests/baseline_census.rs`, beside the pair census it already holds — the standing rule is that a census has one executable home and every other site points at it, and this row's own history above is why. It hands each pair's swap to `compare` and requires an empty `Report`, so a re-cut that makes a swap gate-visible fails there and names the pair and the finding; it then requires the two recoverable slacks to agree exactly, so a sub-tolerance drift reds before the gate-visible one does; and it requires that no pair is separated by `name`, so the day a scene carrying a pair becomes document-built the failure says that `C15` has become dischargeable for those pairs and what to do about it. The disjointness above has its home in the same file.

### Why this stays open

The defect is live: the CSV still cannot tell those faces apart, and the consumer-side join still keys on the ordinal. What closes it is corpus-side — a scene that carries a pair becoming document-built, so that the sweep has an evaluation to take names from — and Ev's ruling on `D201` (*"it's a demo so it doesn't matter much"*) scopes that as not urgent, which is not the same as done.
