## For Ev

**Recommendation (likely).** Mesh a ruled face (a cone or a cylinder) as **strips**, in the one lane the plan already aims at. A *strip* is the part of the face between two neighbouring rulings, where a *ruling* is a straight line of the surface: on a cone, a line from the apex down to the rim. Each boundary point on a rim is joined to its ruling partner by a fixed (constrained) edge. On a face that holds its apex, the partner is the apex. On a frustum (a cone cut off below the apex), it is the other rim's point at the same angle. A strip face lays no interior lattice: no grid points and no rows. Every triangle then sits inside one strip.

The size bound needs no new theorem, just one fact. Two rulings of a cone both pass through the apex, so they lie in one plane. Any triangle whose corners lie on two neighbouring rulings is therefore inside the fan triangle (apex, rim point i, rim point i+1). Its distance from the surface is at most that fan triangle's, and `sizing::cone_grid_step` already bounds that at δ. A cylinder works the same way, with two parallel rulings and the rectangle between them. A point that splits a side sits on that side's ruling, so it is covered as well.

Until that is built: send a cone face that holds its apex and has a split side to the chart lane, which is measured to mesh it certified. Do not go back to main's behaviour.

**Premise check (sure).** The problem is stated correctly, with three corrections.
1. **Where the fault is.** It is in the triangulation, not in the lattice. The rectangle lane builds its Delaunay triangulation in the raw chart, where the apex is a whole row of copies. Its only rule at the apex drops triangles that use two apex copies. Nothing stops a triangle made of one apex copy plus two points on one ruling, which has no area. The lattice only supplies those two points. The chart lane's own header already describes this exact failure, and that is why its cone arm triangulates in the development (the cone unrolled flat). So one fact about the cone's chart (which flat plane to triangulate in) is read by one lane and ignored by the other, even though both read the same chart row (`chart_table::AnalyticRow::development`).
2. **The zero-area triangle is one symptom of a bigger gap.** The open row `a-cone-face-with-a-split-side-has-no-worst-triangle-theorem` says the lattice is kept on a split side "because a triangle on two side points and a far rim point spans several columns". That is the same missing rule: nothing keeps triangles inside one strip. The lattice was laid to make up for it, and the lattice is what now produces the zero-area triangles. With strips, the lattice and that row's missing theorem both go away.
3. **The cylinder has the same gap, unguarded (measured).** A revolved cylinder face whose straight side is split refuses `CertificateExceeded` at every δ in the rectangle lane. The bound it reports is 0.20, 0.14 and 0.13 at δ 0.1, 0.01 and 0.003: it barely shrinks as δ does, so the fault is in the shape of the triangulation. The cylinder arm always returns no rows (`(nu, 1)`) and has no bare-side check. The existing test `a_multiply_carried_iso_side…` counts these refusals and does not fail on them.

**The options, each as the code would end up (§4: final state only).**

*A. The strips live in the chart lane, and every cone face routes there (recommended, likely).* The chart-table row for a cone or cylinder says "ruled: no rows; rulings with both ends on the boundary are constraints". The chart lane's cone arm stops laying rows on a face bounded by rims, rulings and the apex, and it sizes at δ by the fan-triangle bound above, so the headroom factor (`UNPROVED_HEADROOM`, which sizes at half δ) does not apply to it.
- What it makes true: one lane and one apex rule. A zero-area triangle cannot be built, because the development's exact predicates never form a triangle along one ruling. The split-side theorem row closes. A ruled face moved over carries about as many triangles as the rectangle lane emits today (one per rim chord, plus one per side point), so the density objection to unit 6 disappears for these two surface kinds.
- What it leaves open: a frustum whose two rims are chorded at different counts and whose side is split. Not every rim point then has a partner on the other rim, so the strips do not cover the whole face (unsure, not measured). That case keeps today's lattice and certificate until it has its own rule. A face that spans both nappes (both halves of the double cone) still refuses in the development (`BothNappes`). In the raw chart, the strip rule would handle it with one apex copy per ruling, which shows the rule does not depend on the development (likely).
- Reversible: yes. It is a per-chart rule and a route.

*B. The strips live in the rectangle lane's cone and cylinder arms (defensible, a clear second).* This fixes every measured case, and the rectangle lane's triangle counts stay as they are. But two lanes each keep their own cone apex rule and their own way of reading a chart. That is exactly the duplication that caused this defect, and the plan already retires this lane. Choose it only if unit 6 is turned down.

*C. Keep the lattice and add a local rule against apex triangles* (insert points on the ruling, or drop the zero-area triangles). Rejected. Dropping a triangle opens a hole in the mesh. Adding points changes the boundary that the neighbouring face shares, which creates a T-junction (a vertex on one face's edge that the other face lacks). The split-side face would still have no size theorem.
