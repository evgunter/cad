1. **Rows 1 and 28: they are not legitimate `Centre` reads.** I take the other report's position and withdraw my own count, which had them as mints.
   - **Why.** The `RevolvedPoint` scaffold is a second statement of the same edge, which certification then checks against the carrier. It describes nothing new. On a circle held on its arc, it would state the axis through the derived centre. Certification would then meter the scaffold against the carrier at the radius's rounding, about u·r. On exactly the far-centred circles the arc placement exists for, that is wider than ε. The likely result is a refusal, not a wrong answer, but it is still the derived centre used as a position by a side route.
   - **What the scaffold is built from instead.** Either the conic's position (the edge's own start point, revolved), or, where the edge lies on a cylinder, cone, sphere or torus, that surface's stored axis. The surface's axis is a datum of that surface, not one derived from the circle.
   - **The caller list then reads:** reporters (the STEP writer, the sketch written back at row 57, a description shown to a person) and mints of a new entity's datum (rows 38, 39, 51 and the torus arm of 61). A conic's own edge description is never on it.
   - **The likely objection.** If the scaffold's revolve form genuinely needs a point on the axis and the edge lies on no surface of revolution, that point is a property of the circle's whole locus. It is legitimate only for a circle held at its centre. On an arc-held circle the scaffold should be refused rather than rounded. That is the minting unit's acceptance condition, and it should be written onto the row that mints the import's circle.
