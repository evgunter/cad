cone-axis-winding — fix-pass item C (DevelopedMerge at ε 1e-12): REPRODUCED. The cure is not a few lines; I am holding it for your ruling.

Bodies: the reviewer's frustum wall (1,0),(3,0),(2,1),(1.5,1), and an axis-touching frustum (0,0),(2,0),(1,1),(0,1), both Full, placed obliquely. A cone face's ruling (or seam) is split at 1e-9 of its parameter range, at CAD_TOLERANCE_EPS=1e-12. Every such cell at δ 0.1, 0.01 and 0.001 refuses SphereTrimShape { DevelopedMerge }, on both cone faces of each body. Unsplit, they mesh.

What merges: `trimmed::developed` cuts the slant radius to 27 significant bits and the direction to 26, so every product is exact and every point of one chart column lies on one exact line through the origin. That is the rule that makes the original T1 triangle impossible. Two points of one ruling whose slant radii agree to within 2⁻²⁷ of the radius (≈7.5e-9 relative) floor to the same developed point. A split vertex 1e-9 of the length from the ruling's end is such a pair: the split vertex and the end vertex become one CDT vertex, and `developed_merge` refuses it as DevelopedMerge (two mesh vertices the CDT would hold as one). At the default ε, `split_edge` refuses a split that near an end, so the case only exists at 1e-12. On the base these faces were on the rectangle lane, whose chart CDT has no cut, so they meshed.

Cure options I see, none of them small:
(1) Give the radius more bits for a face whose points need them. The exactness needs bits(r) + bits(dir) ≤ 53, so this costs direction bits, and two columns closer in angle than the cut would then merge instead. A per-face split chosen from the face's own closest pair along a ruling and closest pair of columns is a new decision with a refusal of its own when neither fits. Size: medium. It changes the development's cut for every cone face, so every chart-lane cone patch's bytes move.
(2) Keep a point that merges with another of its own ruling as a separate CDT vertex at its unfloored position, accepting that this one point is off the exact line. That gives up the exact-collinearity rule for one point, which is what made the T1 triangle impossible, so no.
(3) Route back: a class face whose development would merge two points stays on the rectangle lane. That is a new rectangle-lane case the designers' answer deleted, and its T1-like triangles come back. No.
(4) Let it stand as a refusal at ε 1e-12 only: the face that meshed on the base refuses typed and names the merge. It is a stop by the spec unless you rule it a residual. If so I would file it (P3) with the mechanism, these bodies and the 2⁻²⁷ bound, and pin one cell.
My recommendation is (4) now, with (1) as the filed cure; it is a design choice about the development's cut. Your call, or Ev's.
