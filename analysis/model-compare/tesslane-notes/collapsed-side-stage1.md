THE CURE, AND ITS LAYER: the polygon, in `trimmed.rs`. No certificate change.
1. Identify the singular vertices of a spline face from the home, `singular_points(surface)` (its `Side` points). The same `singular_vertices` walk the analytic arm runs, with the decision passed in.
2. `flat_points` then keeps the copy as it does at a pole, so the column is a side of the polygon. Shared with the analytic arms, not a copy.
3. Walk that side through the vertex at the face's chord step: pieces = ⌈|Δfree|/h⌉, with h the face's whole-patch steps `row.steps(face_target)`. That is exactly the rim-chord rule of `[mesh.sizing]`, applied to the one boundary side that has no edge. On the hinge, the collapsed column gets 9 points, the same count as the strut edge on that wall's opposite column. This step is spline-only, and the reason is the certificates: the analytic sphere/spindle certificate reads 3-D and the cone's has no azimuth at the apex, so their pole sides need no point between, while the spline's reads the chart box. Each inserted point lies on the collapsed side, so S(uv) = P and the parametric certificate stays sound. The CDT triangle between two copies has two corners on P and is dropped by the existing emit rule; the remaining fan triangles are one lattice step tall.
- A vertex on TWO collapsed sides at once (a corner walk) gets no inserted points: the lerp would leave the side. That shape keeps the copy-only behaviour, is sound, and refuses typed if δ is not met. No door builds it.

WHAT IT MOVES: only faces with a collapsed side. Those are the walls of a pinned corner, a hinge or a lune; no other face takes the new branch, and I will show that with patch digests.
