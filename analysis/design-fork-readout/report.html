Opus vs Fable designers: which first report did Ev's decision take?

First readout of the design-fork experiment · 230 rows of docs/DESIGN-FORK-LOG.md (24 Sep – 30 Oct 2026), 212 decided · two independent blinded coding passes · Beta(1,1) priors throughout

Ev's decisions lean slightly toward the Opus designer's first report, but the lean is not established. Where Ev's decision took exactly one designer's first recommendation, it was Opus's 56% [44, 68] of the time, with 85% posterior probability that the true share is above even. Counting every decided fork, the lean shrinks to 52% [43, 61]. The more consistent differences are in how the two designers work: Opus more often challenges ratified text, and Fable more often states high confidence.

What "first report" means here

The protocol records each designer's recommendation before either sees the other's report. Most forks then go through reconciliation, and the PR carries the converged position: Ev accepted it as carried in 75% of decided forks, amended it in 22% and rejected it in 4%. So "Ev took Opus's first report" mostly means that reconciliation converged on Opus's opening position and Ev then approved it. This measures whose first instinct survived, not a preference Ev expressed between two named options. Ev never saw the model names.

1. The head-to-head

A row counts as head-to-head when Ev's decision matches one designer's first recommendation and not the other's, as judged by blinded coders comparing the ev cell with the two first reports. Pass 1 found 43 decided Opus's way and 37 Fable's; pass 2 found 48 and 33. The posterior below is the equal mixture of the two passes' Beta posteriors, so disagreement between the coders widens it rather than being settled by fiat.

Taking each designer on its own, Ev's decision took Opus's first report (alone or shared) in 46% [40, 53] of decided forks and Fable's in 41% [34, 49]. The difference, +4.9 points [-5.3, +15.0], includes zero. Neither designer's first report is what Ev ends up with most of the time. Forks are usually multi-part, and the final answer is assembled.

2. Robustness

  • Label position is a clean control. Ev took label A 47% [36, 59] of the time. The urandom labels work, and no position effect is hiding a model effect.
  • The log's own match cell leans further toward Opus (63% [50, 75]). That cell was written after unblinding, so it is worth checking for bias. Its one-sided cells mostly agree with the coders: of the 57 rows the log credits to one designer alone, blinded pass 1 keeps 48 on that designer and pass 2 keeps 46 of 47. The difference comes from rows the log calls "both" (usually "both, after reconciliation"). The coders re-split those against the first reports: 10 to Opus and 15 to Fable in pass 1, 13 and 13 in pass 2. That split is even, so it dilutes the lean rather than reversing it. There is no sign that the unblinded writer shaded the cell toward either model. The forks the log itself reads as clean wins favour Opus more than the marginal ones do.
  • Dropping the protocol deviations (row 2, whose blinding broke, and row 212, with no byte drawn) changes nothing: 56% [44, 67].
  • Coder reliability is good. The Opus-run and Fable-run coding passes agree on 88% of match codes (Cohen's κ 0.85), 96% on first-report agreement (κ 0.93) and 97% on reconciliation movement (κ 0.95). Their head-to-head counts differ (43:37 vs 48:33) mostly on rows one pass calls "part of each".

Over time

The two passes disagree on the direction of any drift. The odds ratio per week is 0.98 [0.70, 1.38] in pass 1 and 1.26 [0.90, 1.80] in pass 2. Both intervals contain 1, so there is no trend.

3. How the designers differ

The fields each first report records (confidence, framing rejected, ratified text challenged) separate the two models more cleanly than Ev's decisions do. Each estimate is taken over the rows where the two designers differed on that field.

  • Opus challenges ratified text more. Opus did so on 74% of its first reports and Fable on 67%. When exactly one challenged, it was Opus 70% [54, 82], the clearest difference in the data. Challenging does not reliably win: on head-to-head forks Ev went the challenger's way 57% [34, 78] of the time.
  • Both designers reject the framing almost always (Opus 91%, Fable 87%). The field barely discriminates. In the few head-to-head forks where only one rejected it, Ev sided with the rejecter 73% [42, 93], from about nine rows.
  • Fable states high confidence more. Nearly every report says "likely". When the two confidences differed, the higher one was Fable's on 80% of forks. 4. What this does and does not show

  • A weak lean, not a result. At about 80 head-to-head forks the interval still spans roughly 44–68%. If the true share is near 56%, ruling out "even" at the 95% level takes roughly 270 head-to-head forks, about three times the current count. At the current pace of about two head-to-head forks a day, that is around three more months.
  • The outcome is Ev's judgment, not ground truth. "Ev took Opus's report" says whose answer Ev chose, not whose design held up later. A follow-up could score decided forks by whether the chosen design was later revised.
  • Forks are not independent. Several rows share a program and an orchestrator, and some forks follow from earlier ones. The Beta model treats them as exchangeable, so the intervals are somewhat too narrow.
  • Reconciliation is part of the treatment. Because Ev usually decides on the converged recommendation, a designer that argues its first position persuasively in reconciliation will score well here even if Ev would have chosen differently between the raw first reports.

Method

parse_log.py splits the 230 table rows (some fork numbers repeat after merge collisions, so rows are keyed by line). blind_extract.py drops the A/B column, redacts every model name and fails if any survives. Eight agents then coded the four extract chunks against CODING-PROMPT.md, a full pass by Opus coders and a full pass by Fable coders. None could see the mapping. key.py reads the mapping from each row's A/B column, checks it against the urandom byte's parity (no contradictions), and is the only file that names the models. analyze.py joins the two and fits every proportion as a Beta(1,1)–binomial and the outcome mix as a Dirichlet. Each estimate is reported pooled over both passes. Everything lives on analysis/design-fork/readout-1.

Off-file readout under the design-fork protocol's rule 7: kept on an analysis/ branch, not summarised anywhere agents read. An orchestrator with a design fork in flight should not read it.
