Is there enough data?

I checked this before fitting anything. The answer differs by question.

Fable vs Opus as reviewersenough

40 Fable and 49 Opus fair reviews, including 18 concurrent units where both models read the same frozen head. The pairs carry the result, and they are all H-lettered units.

Fable vs Opus as implementersenough to rule out a large gap

30 Fable and 39 Opus fair implementer halves. Wide intervals; and the measurement depends on which model reviewed (see the interaction below).

Token costnot yet comparable

Tokens are recorded in about a third of phases, in three incompatible forms (per-turn, cumulative per agent, per session). Dollar cost and wall clock are usable; token volume is descriptive only.

Design-fork designers (5.2 era)thin

35 two-designer and 15 three-designer forks since the release. Ev took both designers' shared answer in most; only 7 + 7 two-designer forks discriminate.

What the rows say

  1. Fable 5.2 is the more thorough reviewer. Per review it raises about 2.44 [1.50–4.48] times the MAJORs Opus 5.5 does (34 vs 15 in total), adjusted for the implementer and the letter, and the excess holds for DEMONSTRATED MAJORs alone. It returns NOT-MERGEABLE-AS-IS on 11/40 reviews against Opus's 4/49.
  2. On the same code, Fable finds what Opus misses, rarely the reverse. In the 12 concurrent pairs with a correspondence count, Fable raised more unilateral findings in 11: 140 against 63 in total. Unilateral MAJORs: 8 Fable, 1 Opus. Fable also grades a little harder: where both raised one finding at different severities, Fable put it higher in 9 units and Opus in 4 (5 against 3 at the MAJOR line). But in the pairs Fable's MAJOR excess is 16 against 8, and 7 of those 8 come from findings Opus never raised, so the gap is mostly coverage. A blind severity pilot (below) finds no long tail of low-value Fable findings.
  3. Fable's review costs more. About 1.4× the wall clock (median 56 vs 37 min) and about 2× the dollars (median $10.75 vs $4.72), for roughly the same token volume: the dollar gap is price per token, not reading more. Within pairs: time ×1.26, cost ×2.12 (n=9), tokens ×1.08 (n=9).
  4. As implementers, no difference shows. MAJORs received per review: Fable/Opus 0.96 [0.52–1.80]. Time and cost intervals straddle 1. Fable's code scores slightly higher on the reviewer's idiom rubric, the only implementer interval that clears zero.
  5. But the implementer comparison depends on who reviews. Opus reviewers raised almost no MAJORs on Fable's code; Fable reviewers raised the most on it. Judged by Opus reviews, Fable writes the cleaner code; judged by Fable reviews, Opus does. This is post hoc and the cells are small; it says the implementer question cannot be read off pooled MAJOR counts.
  6. Design forks: even. Where Ev's decision matched only one designer's first report on a two-designer fork, it was Fable 7 times and Opus 7 (90% interval for Fable's share 30%–70%).

Harsher, or more thorough?

A follow-up pilot on the three concurrent pairs whose full reports and blind coding survive (PRs 6972, 7203, 7225; all H). Each of the 92 underlying defects was rewritten into one neutral template with severity, confidence and attribution stripped, then graded and ranked blind by four raters (two Opus, two Sonnet: the Fable raters hit the usage limit, and a model that is neither reviewer is a fair stand-in).

  1. No long tail. Fable's unilateral findings grade like the ones both reviewers raised, and no lower than Opus's unilateral ones (within-unit permutation: rank p = 0.40, grade p = 0.48). Each unit on its own shows the same.
  2. Opus is the lenient grader, not Fable the harsh one. Against the blind grade, Fable's own labels sit 0.25 grade points low and Opus's 0.45 low. On the 39 shared findings both labelled, Fable was higher in 12, Opus in 5, the same in 22, and Fable was nearer the blind grade (mean absolute error 0.42 vs 0.56).
  3. The raters agree (Spearman 0.84–0.99 on ranks; all four within one grade on 95% of findings), and the Opus raters did not favour Opus-found findings.

Three units is a pilot. The raters take each finding's facts as true, so a wrong finding is not penalised here. Materials and scripts: severity-pilot/.

Caveats

  • Severity is the reviewer's own label. A MAJOR is what the review called a MAJOR. DEMONSTRATED narrows that to findings backed by a probe, a mutant, a compile probe or a red row, and the result survives it; the orchestrator's TAKEN/ARGUED DOWN calls are unblinded and argued down 2.5% of Opus's and 4% of Fable's findings, so they do not discriminate.
  • Orchestrators differ. 43 rows were run by a Fable 5.2 orchestrator and 30 by Opus 5.5. Briefs, adjudication and row-writing pass through that orchestrator; it is the same for both arms within a unit, but it is not constant across units.
  • Extraction. The rows are free-form; four agents extracted them to rows.json under SCHEMA.md, and I spot-checked rows against the source. Every recorded byte matches its model (even → Opus); 70 of 122 bytes are even, unremarkable for 1:1 draws.
  • EMBED drew Opus thirteen times running (bytes on analysis/model-compare/embed-2026-11-17; about 1 in 8,000 by chance). Its later draws are mixed and the bytes are recorded, so its seven rows stay in. Dropping them leaves the reviewer MAJOR ratio at 2.6 and the implementer ratio at 1.0.
  • Many comparisons, small n. Intervals are per-estimand. The reviewer gap clears 1 on several correlated measures; the interaction and the rubric difference are single post-hoc estimates.
  • Escapes are too few to use. Four rows carry later escapes (6959 and 6963 Opus/Opus, 7143 Fable/Fable, 7146 Fable code under an Opus review, the last a roster entry rather than a defect). None was raised by its review.
  • Waits are not subtracted from wall clock (slow-set runs, CI polls, usage-limit outages). Rows written before the 2026-11-17 outage rule recorded outage interruptions as either-arm-alike and stay counted.
