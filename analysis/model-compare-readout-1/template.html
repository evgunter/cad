What the rows say

  1. Fable 5.2 is the more thorough reviewer. Per review it raises about {{MAJ_RR}} times the MAJORs Opus 5.5 does ({{MAJ_F}} vs {{MAJ_O}} in total), adjusted for the implementer and the letter, and the excess holds for DEMONSTRATED MAJORs alone. It returns NOT-MERGEABLE-AS-IS on {{NM_F}} reviews against Opus's {{NM_O}}.
  2. On the same code, Fable finds what Opus misses, rarely the reverse. In the {{UNI_N}} concurrent pairs with a correspondence count, Fable raised more unilateral findings in {{UNI_WIN}}: {{UNI_F}} against {{UNI_O}} in total. Unilateral MAJORs: {{UMAJ_F}} Fable, {{UMAJ_O}} Opus. Fable also grades a little harder: where both raised one finding at different severities, Fable put it higher in 9 units and Opus in 4 (5 against 3 at the MAJOR line). But in the pairs Fable's MAJOR excess is 16 against 8, and 7 of those 8 come from findings Opus never raised, so the gap is mostly coverage.
  3. Fable's review costs more. About 1.4× the wall clock (median {{RV_T_F}} vs {{RV_T_O}} min) and about 2× the dollars (median ${{RV_C_F}} vs ${{RV_C_O}}), for roughly the same token volume: the dollar gap is price per token, not reading more. Within pairs: time ×{{PT_RATIO}}, cost ×{{PC_RATIO}} (n={{PC_N}}), tokens ×{{PTOK_RATIO}} (n={{PTOK_N}}).
  4. As implementers, no difference shows. MAJORs received per review: Fable/Opus {{IMP_MAJ_RR}}. Time and cost intervals straddle 1. Fable's code scores slightly higher on the reviewer's idiom rubric, the only implementer interval that clears zero.
  5. But the implementer comparison depends on who reviews. Opus reviewers raised almost no MAJORs on Fable's code; Fable reviewers raised the most on it. Judged by Opus reviews, Fable writes the cleaner code; judged by Fable reviews, Opus does. This is post hoc and the cells are small; it says the implementer question cannot be read off pooled MAJOR counts.
  6. Design forks: even. Where Ev's decision matched only one designer's first report on a two-designer fork, it was Fable {{DF_F}} times and Opus {{DF_O}} (90% interval for Fable's share {{DF_LO}}–{{DF_HI}}).

Caveats

  • Severity is the reviewer's own label. A MAJOR is what the review called a MAJOR. DEMONSTRATED narrows that to findings backed by a probe, a mutant, a compile probe or a red row, and the result survives it; the orchestrator's TAKEN/ARGUED DOWN calls are unblinded and argued down 2.5% of Opus's and 4% of Fable's findings, so they do not discriminate.
  • Orchestrators differ. {{ORCH_F}} rows were run by a Fable 5.2 orchestrator and {{ORCH_O}} by Opus 5.5. Briefs, adjudication and row-writing pass through that orchestrator; it is the same for both arms within a unit, but it is not constant across units.
  • Extraction. The rows are free-form; four agents extracted them to rows.json under SCHEMA.md, and I spot-checked rows against the source. Every recorded byte matches its model (even → Opus); {{DRAWS_E}} of {{DRAWS_N}} bytes are even, unremarkable for 1:1 draws.
  • EMBED drew Opus thirteen times running (bytes on analysis/model-compare/embed-2026-11-17; about 1 in 8,000 by chance). Its later draws are mixed and the bytes are recorded, so its seven rows stay in. Dropping them leaves the reviewer MAJOR ratio at 2.6 and the implementer ratio at 1.0.
  • Many comparisons, small n. Intervals are per-estimand. The reviewer gap clears 1 on several correlated measures; the interaction and the rubric difference are single post-hoc estimates.
  • Escapes are too few to use. Four rows carry later escapes (6959 and 6963 Opus/Opus, 7143 Fable/Fable, 7146 Fable code under an Opus review, the last a roster entry rather than a defect). None was raised by its review.
  • Waits are not subtracted from wall clock (slow-set runs, CI polls, usage-limit outages). Rows written before the 2026-11-17 outage rule recorded outage interruptions as either-arm-alike and stay counted.
