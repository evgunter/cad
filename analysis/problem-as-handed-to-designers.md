THE PROBLEM

On 2026-11-21 Ev approved (PR 7194, merged) that every conic carrier has one stored form. The ratified sentence, in `crates/geom-core/README.md` ("Float rounding"): "A stored form that loses digits is re-minted in a well-conditioned form: a conic is stored anchored on the arc it serves, its parameter zero at the anchor and its centre derived, never stored." The reason for it is measured and not in dispute: a point of a conic computed as `centre + size·direction` carries rounding of the centre's distance, so a shallow arc of a far-centred conic loses digits, and verdicts near it are wrong or refuse (`work/oneround/a-conics-point-is-evaluated-from-its-far-centre-not-its-vertex.md`). The decision record is `docs/DESIGN-FORK-LOG.md` row 294 and PR 7194's body (read it with the GitHub tools if you have them, or at `git show origin/analysis/design-fork/conic-born-form:analysis/reports/cbf-pr-body.md`); it carries two earlier designers' reports, each of which lists, under what it did not check, the form's behaviour at the `Interval` scalar and the readers of a conic's centre.

A lane then built it and measured before changing any expectation. The build: nothing records how a conic was made; a constructor handed a centre (`Circle::new(frame, r)`, `Ellipse::new(frame, a, b)`) stores the anchor `centre + u_ref·a`, summed once; every conic evaluates from its anchor; `Conic::center()` and `Conic::frame()` derive the centre on each read. The evidence, facts only: branch `analysis/oneround/conic-vertex-probes`, directory `analysis/oneround/conic-vertex/step-b-one-form/` (`README.md`, `rows.txt`, `b_payloads.txt`). The code: commit `e04a87227` on branch `oneround/anchored-conic-one-form`; the step before it, which keeps two ways for a conic to be made and anchors only the kernel's cone sections, is `3193e7c97` on `oneround/anchored-conic` (both stacked on `onehome/carrier-frames`, PR 7131, which changes how every carrier holds its frame: read its body too).

Of 17,465 tests, 45 move. About 30 are last-bit re-baselines the decision expected. The rest:
1. At the `Interval` scalar and the symbolic scalar, a conic built from a centre is wider. A reader of the centre gets `(c + u·a) − u·a`. About ten tests that built now refuse or miss a width bar (transversality enclosures past the zero band, a rung that stalls, a profile validation undecided), and at the symbolic scalar `c + u·r − u·r` is not recognised as `c`.
2. At `f64`, the derived centre is `fl(fl(c + u·a) − u·a)`, not `c`, so whatever states a conic from its centre is no longer the curve the evaluator draws, by up to a rounding of the semi-axis. This is a class, not a site: covers, extent bounds, chart images, chart-frame readers, the inverse `param_near`. One instance is unsound: on an ellipse of semi-axis 1e7, a short arc's cover does not hold the arc's own point, by 4.2e-10 m. The lane counts about 90 production sites that read a conic's centre (or its frame's origin) as a datum.
3. A constructor handed a centre has no arc to anchor on, so it anchors at the `u_ref` vertex; an arc near another vertex of a long ellipse is then summed across a semi-axis (an end misses its vertex by 6.5e-13 where the centre form, re-seamed to that vertex, held 1e-14).
Separately measured and already settled, for context: with no stored phase, re-seaming an anchored ellipse widened `Interval` enclosures by a third; storing the anchor's anomaly removed that, and `3193e7c97` is narrower at `Interval` than the centre form on the kernel's cone sections.

WHAT NEEDS DECIDING, AND WHY

What a conic IS to the code that reads it, given both sets of measurements: the far-centre loss that motivated the ratified sentence, and what deriving the centre costs every reader that wants one. Concretely the answer has to say: what is stored and what is derived; what a constructor handed a centre does; what a reader that needs a conic's centre, axis or frame reads, at `f64`, at `Interval` and at the symbolic scalar; which of the things the tree gets today by bit agreement (a cover holding its arc, a certificate equal to a fresh one, the STEP round trip's fixed point, an exact centre for a circle authored at one) are contracts and which are accidents; and whether the ratified sentence stands as written, stands with different words, or should change. It is back with designers because the sentence was ratified without these measurements, and because the lane cannot land the form as built: it would turn certified builds into refusals and ship an unsound cover.
