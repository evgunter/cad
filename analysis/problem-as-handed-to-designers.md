## What needs deciding

A sector is the piece of a face's neighbourhood at a vertex between two bounds; a bound is an edge's departure from the vertex, or a divider. The sector machinery (`topo::star::walk`, `topo::bound_side`, `topo::link` and `link::second_order`, `topo::sector_face`, `topo::sector_shape`, `geom_brep::sector_shape`, `geom_brep::conic_bend`, and their consumers in `topo::boolean` and `topo::splitting`) decides which side of a face a bound lies on, whether a corner holds, how sectors nest.

A bound reaches those predicates as ONE VECTOR: a direction scaled by a lever's length. For a line edge that vector is real geometry (the chord to its far vertex). For a curved edge it is the departure's unit tangent times the edge's extent lever (`geom_brep::conic_bend::ConicDeparture::of`: `dir: tangent.normalize() * extent.length()`; `topo::bound_side::BoundSupport::point`; `topo::star::walk::Bound::ray`), which ends on the tangent line, not on the edge: no point of the body is there. The predicates then read the one vector two ways: its norm as a lever, and its direction or its end point as geometry. A unit that was converting "sizes read back out of a lever" (PR 7628, in review; its body lists what it left) stopped at these sites, because the honest conversion changes what a sector bound holds. Its reading of the sites, which you should check against the tree:
