# G2: the line site decided two-sided

G1's build (the line site), with four changes:

- **(i) Every levered decision is decided two-sided as the run's own verdict.**
  A definite sign stands on the bound from below. A Zero stands only where
  the bound from above also reads Zero. A disagreement is refused in band
  (`tl_shadow::two_sided`, forced through the decision recorder).
- **(ii) The line site's bound from below** takes, besides the vertices, each
  boundary edge's farthest point from the line (`tl_far_from_line`):
  - on a line: its ends;
  - on a circle or an ellipse: the critical points of the squared distance;
  - elsewhere: 17 samples.
- **(iii) A truth for faces that are cylinder or cone walls along a ruling**:
  the boundary's maximum, by convexity along rulings (below).
- **(iv) Config B only (`TL_ABOVE_CLOSED`)**: where every boundary edge of
  both faces is read exactly and both faces are planes, cylinders or cones,
  the bound from above is the boundary maximum itself, rather than the
  covers.

The suites are topo, step-import, sweep and editor-core at 1e-9, and topo
plus step-import ("core") and sweep at 1e-12 and 1e-6, as in G1. Test
outcomes are judged against the middle-site base runs of the same build
(`m-nextest-*`).

## What (ii) reads, and what (iii)'s truth rests on

**(ii) is not a closed form.** On a conic, the squared distance from a line,
|(P(θ) − O) × d|², is a trigonometric polynomial of degree two in θ. It has
at most two local maxima per period. They are bracketed on 64 samples of the
arc and refined by golden section, to f64 resolution in the value (the
maximum is flat to first order). Two maxima that fall inside one sample
interval would be missed. A degree-two polynomial makes that possible only
where its two maxima nearly merge, and there the value lost is second
order. Each point read lies on the edge, so the read stays a bound from
below whether or not the maximum is found. On a curve that is not a line or
a conic, the read is 17 samples and is marked inexact. There is then no
truth, and config B's closed bound from above is not used.

**(iii)'s argument.** The distance from a line is a convex function of the
point: it is the norm of an affine map. On a ruling segment, a convex
function's maximum is at one of the segment's ends. Every point of a bounded,
closed face that is a cylinder or cone wall lies on a ruling segment inside
the face whose ends lie on the face's boundary. So the face's maximum is the
maximum over its boundary. A plane is ruled in every direction, so the same
argument covers plane faces with boundary edges of any kind.

- **Verified on paper, not by machine.** No test compares this truth with an
  independent maximiser over the face interior.
- **One hole, found while G2 ran: a cone's apex.** A ruling on a cone ends
  at the apex. The apex is a boundary point only where it is a vertex of the
  face. On a cone cap whose apex is inside the face, the boundary maximum can
  miss the apex and read below the truth. A guard now marks such reads
  inexact: no truth, and no closed bound from above in config B. It counts
  them as `apex-not-a-vertex`. It is in effect from config A's 1e-6 runs on,
  and for all of config B. Config A at 1e-9 and 1e-12 ran without it. There
  the guard only affects which reads are labelled with a truth; config A's
  bound from above is the cover's, so no served verdict depended on it.
  At 1e-6 core, 7,234 cone reads were excluded by the guard.
- **Rims that are not circles.** Ellipse rims (a cylinder or cone cut
  obliquely) are conics, so (ii) reads them as above and the truth stands.
  Rims that are intersection curves or splines are read by 17 samples. They
  carry no truth, and in config B they keep the cover's bound from above.

**What "against the truth" checks in config A.** On exact reads the truth is
the boundary maximum, which is also (ii)'s bound from below. So "equal"
confirms that the two-sided rule served what the true lever serves. It is
not an independent check of (ii). The independent checks are:
- above < below never occurs (0 reads, in every run);
- no served verdict differs from the truth's verdict (0, in every run).

join's verdict is served from below.

Taken on an idle box with the shadow off (no `TL_SHADOW`), so the two-sided
machinery does not run, and only the line site's extra edge reads differ
between "line" (G1) and "g2" (G1 plus (ii)). The set is the 764 tests of
topo, sweep and editor-core whose names say `sym`, `interval` or `dual`, at
1e-9. Each configuration ran twice; per test, the faster of the two runs is
taken.
